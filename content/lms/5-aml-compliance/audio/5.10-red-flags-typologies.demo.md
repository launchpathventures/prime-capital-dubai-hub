Let me show you two versions of handling red flags during a client interaction. The scenario: a potential client, Mr. Viktor Petrov, walks into the office wanting to purchase a AED 6 million apartment in Palm Jumeirah. He is a Russian national, non-resident, and says he runs a "general trading company" in Moscow.

---

[DEMO: Weak]

Mr. Petrov says: "I want to buy something on the Palm. Cash buyer. I can move fast — I want to close within two weeks."

The consultant says: "That is great. Fast closings are always exciting. Let me pull up some available units. What is your budget?"

Mr. Petrov says: "Around 6 million. Maybe more. Price is not really a factor — I just want the right unit."

The consultant says: "Wonderful. We have some excellent options. Let me show you three properties."

Mr. Petrov says: "One more thing — my assistant will handle all the paperwork. I will not be in Dubai for the signing. He has power of attorney."

The consultant says: "No problem. Just have him bring the POA document and his passport. Let us set up viewings for tomorrow."

Mr. Petrov says: "Can we keep this discreet? I do not want my name on any marketing materials or anything public."

The consultant says: "Of course. We are very professional about client privacy. Let me send you the property details tonight."

[COACH EXPLAINS]

The consultant missed at least seven red flags in this single conversation:

One — Russian national, non-resident. Under Prime Capital's risk table, Russian nationals are high-risk. Non-residents are high-risk. Two high-risk classifications simultaneously.

Two — "Cash buyer." He stated he wants to pay cash for a AED 6 million property. High-value cash is a primary ML risk factor in real estate.

Three — "I can move fast — close within two weeks." Strong interest in completing quickly without good cause is a transaction red flag.

Four — "Price is not really a factor." Not interested in better price or payment terms is a red flag — legitimate buyers negotiate.

Five — "My assistant will handle all the paperwork. I will not be in Dubai." Non-face-to-face transaction via a representative is an EDD trigger, and actively avoiding personal contact is a natural person red flag.

Six — requests for secrecy. "Keep this discreet" and "I do not want my name on anything public" — makes unusual requests, especially relating to secrecy.

Seven — general trading company. General trading is classified as high-risk in Prime Capital's entity table.

Seven red flags. Not one. Not two. Seven. And the consultant responded to every single one with enthusiasm rather than scrutiny.

---

[DEMO: Strong]

Mr. Petrov says: "I want to buy something on the Palm. Cash buyer. I can move fast — I want to close within two weeks."

The consultant pauses mentally and begins cataloguing: Russian national. Non-resident. Cash buyer for a high-value property. Wants to close fast. That is four flags already.

The consultant says: "Thank you, Mr. Petrov. Before we look at properties, I need to understand your situation a bit better — this helps me serve you properly and it is part of our regulatory process."

Mr. Petrov says: "Sure."

The consultant says: "You mentioned you are a cash buyer. Can you give me a sense of where the funds would be coming from? For a purchase of this size, our compliance team will need documented source of funds."

Mr. Petrov says: "My company. General trading — import and export. The money is in a company account."

The consultant notes: general trading, import-export — both high-risk entity types. He says: "Thank you. Which company specifically, and where is it registered?"

Mr. Petrov says: "Petrov Trading LLC. Moscow."

The consultant says: "And the funds — would they come from the Moscow company account directly, or through an intermediary?"

Mr. Petrov says: "Through our Dubai office account. We transfer from Moscow to Dubai, then pay from here."

The consultant notes: cross-border transfer route. He says: "I see. And you mentioned wanting to close quickly — is there a reason for the timeframe?"

Mr. Petrov says: "I am only in the region for another month. I would like this done before I leave."

The consultant says: "Understood. You also mentioned that your assistant would handle the paperwork via power of attorney. Would you be available for any part of the process yourself, or would it be entirely through the assistant?"

Mr. Petrov says: "My assistant handles everything. I do not deal with paperwork."

The consultant notes: complete non-face-to-face via POA. He says: "One more question — you mentioned wanting to keep things discreet. Can you elaborate on what you mean by that?"

Mr. Petrov says: "I just do not want my name publicly associated with properties. Business reasons."

The consultant says: "I understand. Mr. Petrov, let me be transparent with you. Based on our initial conversation, our compliance framework will require an enhanced onboarding process for this transaction. This involves additional documentation — company registration, bank statements showing the source of funds, identification for yourself and your assistant, and the power of attorney. Our compliance team will also need to review and approve the transaction before we can proceed. This is standard for purchases of this nature and profile."

Mr. Petrov says: "How long will that take?"

The consultant says: "Typically one to two weeks for the compliance review, depending on how quickly we receive the documentation. I can send you a detailed checklist today."

After the meeting, the consultant immediately contacts the Compliance Officer. He says: "I have a new potential client — Russian national, non-resident, wants to buy a AED 6 million apartment on the Palm with cash from a general trading company via a Moscow-to-Dubai transfer route. He wants to close in two weeks, will not appear personally, uses a POA, does not care about price, and requests secrecy about his identity. I count at least seven red flags. I have not committed to anything — told him enhanced compliance review is needed."

The Compliance Officer says: "Good catch. Do not proceed with viewings until I review this. I want to see the KYC form, the company details, and the screening results before we make any commitment. This may require an ISTR depending on what the screening shows."

[COACH EXPLAINS]

Here is what made this effective:

First, the consultant recognised the red flags in real time. He did not dismiss any of them — he mentally catalogued each one as the conversation progressed.

Second, he asked probing follow-up questions. "Where are the funds coming from?" "Which company?" "Through an intermediary?" "Is there a reason for the timeframe?" Each question gave the client an opportunity to provide a legitimate explanation — and each answer either resolved or deepened the concern.

Third, he did not accuse or confront. His questions were professional and framed as standard procedure. Mr. Petrov did not feel interrogated — he felt engaged.

Fourth, he set expectations honestly. "Enhanced onboarding" and "compliance review" are factual descriptions of what will happen. He did not promise speed he could not deliver.

Fifth, he did not proceed with viewings or any business activity. He stopped at the right point — before any commitment — and escalated to the Compliance Officer with a clear summary of the flags.

Sixth, his escalation to the CO was specific and actionable: he named each red flag, described the client profile, and noted that no commitments had been made.

[KEY INSIGHT]

The most important moment in this scenario was the pause — the mental moment where the consultant shifted from "how can I serve this client" to "what is this client's risk profile." That shift happens in the first 30 seconds of a conversation, and it determines everything that follows. Train yourself to listen for the flags as they appear, not after the meeting is over.

[PRACTICE TIP]

After every initial client meeting, before you do anything else, write down the red flags you observed — even if there are none. This practice builds the habit of systematic assessment. Over time, you will recognise patterns instantly. And when an auditor reviews your files, your documented assessment is your evidence of compliance.
