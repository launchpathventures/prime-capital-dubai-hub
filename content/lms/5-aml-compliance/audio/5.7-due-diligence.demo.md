Let me show you two versions of applying due diligence levels. The scenario: a new client, Dr. Samir Khalili, walks into the office. He is an Iranian national residing in Dubai on a work visa, employed as a surgeon at a private hospital. He wants to purchase a AED 3 million apartment in Dubai Hills for personal use.

---

[DEMO: Weak]

The consultant reviews the KYC form. Iranian national, but he is a salaried professional living in the UAE. The consultant thinks: "He is a doctor — clearly legitimate. Salaried employee, lives here. Standard CDD should be fine."

The consultant says: "Dr. Khalili, everything looks straightforward. You are a surgeon, salaried, living in Dubai — I just need your passport, Emirates ID, and we can start looking at properties."

The consultant completes the standard KYC, runs the screening (no hits), collects basic documents, and proceeds to arrange viewings.

Three months later, the Compliance Officer conducts a file review and flags the case. Dr. Khalili is an Iranian national — automatically classified as high-risk under Prime Capital's individual risk rating table. The file should have had EDD applied: senior management approval, detailed source of funds verification, source of wealth documentation, and semi-annual review scheduled. Instead, it has standard CDD with no enhanced measures.

The consultant receives a compliance notice and the file must be remediated.

[COACH EXPLAINS]

The consultant made a reasonable but incorrect assumption. Dr. Khalili is clearly a legitimate professional — a surgeon at a respected hospital. The natural instinct is to think "this person is obviously not a money launderer."

But AML compliance is not about your personal assessment of someone's character. It is about applying the risk framework consistently. Under Prime Capital's risk rating tables, Iranian nationals residing in the UAE are classified as high-risk — regardless of their employment status, profession, or how legitimate they appear.

The consultant's error was not malicious. But it is exactly the kind of oversight that leads to compliance failures. The risk rating tables exist to remove subjective judgement from the classification process.

---

[DEMO: Strong]

The consultant reviews the KYC form. Iranian national. He immediately checks the risk rating table: Iranian nationals in the UAE are classified as high-risk. This means EDD is required in addition to standard CDD.

The consultant says: "Dr. Khalili, thank you for completing the KYC form. I can see you are a surgeon at Dubai Medical Centre — that is impressive. Before we proceed, I want to be transparent about our process. Because of regulatory requirements related to certain nationalities, our compliance framework requires us to collect some additional documentation. This is not a reflection on you personally — it is a regulatory classification that applies consistently."

Dr. Khalili says: "I understand. Iranian passport, right? I get this a lot."

The consultant says: "I appreciate your understanding. Let me walk you through what I need. First, the standard items: passport and Emirates ID copies, which I have. Your employment contract or a letter from the hospital confirming your position and salary. This will serve as source of funds documentation."

Dr. Khalili says: "I can get a salary certificate from HR this week."

The consultant says: "Perfect. Now, beyond the salary certificate, can you tell me how you will fund the purchase? AED 3 million is a significant amount — will this come from salary savings, a mortgage, or a combination?"

Dr. Khalili says: "Mostly savings. I have been working here for eight years, and I had some savings from before I moved to the UAE. I am also taking a mortgage for about 60 percent."

The consultant says: "So approximately AED 1.2 million in personal savings and AED 1.8 million in mortgage. For the savings portion, I will need bank statements — ideally the last six months — showing the accumulation of funds. For the mortgage, which bank are you using?"

Dr. Khalili says: "Emirates NBD. I have pre-approval."

The consultant says: "Excellent. The mortgage pre-approval letter will be part of the file as well. Now I also need to ask about source of wealth — how have you built your overall financial position over your career?"

Dr. Khalili says: "I have been a surgeon for 22 years. I worked in Tehran for the first 14 years, then moved to Dubai. My salary here is significantly higher than what I earned in Iran. I also received an inheritance when my father passed — he owned commercial property in Isfahan."

The consultant says: "Thank you for sharing that. I will note the career history and the inheritance. Do you have any documentation related to the inheritance — a probate certificate or property transfer document?"

Dr. Khalili says: "I can get the court documents translated. Is that necessary?"

The consultant says: "For the enhanced process, yes — any documentation that supports the source of wealth is helpful. A certified translation would be ideal. Let me also run through a couple more items. Are you or any family member a politically exposed person — someone who holds or has held a senior government or political position?"

Dr. Khalili says: "No. I am a doctor, not a politician."

The consultant says: "Understood. And finally, I need to inform you that as part of our compliance process, your application will need approval from our senior management before we can formally proceed. This is a standard requirement for the enhanced process. It is not a barrier — it is a procedural step."

Dr. Khalili says: "No problem. How long does that take?"

The consultant says: "Typically a few days once I have all the documentation. Let me summarise what I need from you: the salary certificate from your hospital, six months of bank statements for the savings account, the mortgage pre-approval letter, and the translated inheritance documents. Once I have those, I will submit the file for senior management approval and we can start viewing properties."

[COACH EXPLAINS]

Here is what made this effective:

First, the consultant immediately identified the correct risk classification. Iranian national in the UAE equals high-risk — no subjective assessment, no "but he is a doctor" override. The risk rating table was applied as designed.

Second, he was transparent about the enhanced process without being apologetic or accusatory. "Regulatory requirements related to certain nationalities" is honest and professional. The client's response — "I get this a lot" — shows this was not a surprise.

Third, he collected detailed, specific source of funds information. Not just "savings" — but how much, from which account, over what period, and supplemented by what (mortgage). Each component has a corresponding document requirement.

Fourth, he distinguished source of funds from source of wealth and collected both. The 22-year surgical career and the Isfahan inheritance are documented, with supporting evidence requested.

Fifth, he proactively informed the client about senior management approval. No surprises later. The client knows the process and the timeline.

Sixth, he ended with a clear, specific document checklist. The client leaves knowing exactly what is needed and when.

[KEY INSIGHT]

The key insight from this scenario is that EDD does not mean treating the client as a suspect. It means applying a more thorough process — more documentation, more verification, senior management sign-off. Dr. Khalili is very likely a completely legitimate buyer. But the framework requires enhanced measures for his risk profile, and applying them professionally actually builds trust. A sophisticated client respects a firm that follows its own rules.

[PRACTICE TIP]

When you encounter a high-risk classification that seems at odds with the client's apparent legitimacy, resist the temptation to downgrade. The risk tables exist to be applied consistently. If the client is genuinely low-risk, the EDD documentation will confirm that quickly. If they are not, the documentation will reveal it. Either way, the process protects you.
