Let me walk you through four different client scenarios and determine the correct due diligence level for each. This is the decision you will make every time a new client comes through the door.

---

## Scenario 1: The Government Entity

**Client:** Dubai Municipality — a government department purchasing a commercial building for office expansion.

**Step 1: Risk classification.** Government entities are classified as low-risk in Prime Capital's entity risk table.

**Step 2: Check for disqualifying factors.** Is there any suspicion of ML/TF? No. Is the transaction consistent with the entity's profile? Yes — a government department expanding its office space is entirely normal. Are there any unusual payment arrangements? No — funding comes from the government budget.

**Step 3: Determine CDD level.** This client meets all conditions for Simplified Due Diligence: government entity, low risk confirmed, no suspicion.

**Step 4: Apply SDD measures.** Verify identity, but you may do so after establishing the relationship. Reduced frequency of monitoring. Infer purpose from the transaction type (office expansion).

**Step 5: Set review frequency.** Low risk = every 2 years.

**Key point:** SDD is appropriate here. But if anything changes — an unusual payment, a third-party involvement, any indicator of suspicion — you must immediately escalate to standard CDD or EDD.

## Scenario 2: The UAE Resident Salaried Employee

**Client:** Ms. Sarah Jensen, a Danish national, UAE resident for 6 years, working as a marketing director at a multinational in Dubai Internet City. She wants to buy a AED 1.5 million apartment in JVC for personal use.

**Step 1: Risk classification.** Salaried person residing in the UAE — and Denmark is not on the FATF grey or black list. Using Prime Capital's individual risk table: salaried, UAE resident, no high-risk nationality = low risk.

**Step 2: Check for disqualifying factors.** No suspicion. Transaction is consistent with her profile — a salaried professional buying a residential property within a reasonable range for her likely income. No unusual payment methods.

**Step 3: Determine CDD level.** Standard CDD. While the risk classification is low, SDD is typically reserved for government entities and listed companies at Prime Capital. Standard CDD is the default for individual clients.

**Step 4: Apply the 9 CDD measures.** Collect KYC form and 7 required documents. Verify identity from passport and Emirates ID. Screen through Cygnus Scan. Determine purpose — personal residence. Establish source of funds — salary savings and potentially a mortgage.

**Step 5: Set review frequency.** Low risk = every 2 years.

**Key point:** This is a straightforward, low-risk transaction. Standard CDD is sufficient. No EDD triggers are present.

## Scenario 3: The PEP's Family Member

**Client:** Mr. Ahmed Al-Saud, a Saudi national, non-resident, visiting Dubai quarterly. He wants to purchase a AED 8 million villa in Emirates Hills. He is the son of a senior member of the Saudi Shura Council.

**Step 1: Risk classification.** Multiple high-risk factors converge. Non-resident — automatically high-risk. Saudi Shura Council member's son — PEP family member. Emirates Hills villa at AED 8 million — high-value transaction. Using Prime Capital's table: non-resident = high-risk.

**Step 2: Identify PEP status.** The client's father is a senior member of a legislative body — this makes the father a PEP. As the son of a PEP, Mr. Ahmed is classified as a PEP family member. All PEP procedures apply.

**Step 3: Determine CDD level.** Enhanced Due Diligence — mandatory for PEPs, non-residents, and high-value transactions. This is EDD on top of standard CDD, not instead of it.

**Step 4: Apply EDD measures.**
- All 9 standard CDD measures apply
- Detailed source of funds inquiry — which account, what type of income, bank statements
- Source of wealth verification — how has the family accumulated wealth? Independent documentation required
- Senior management approval — mandatory before establishing the relationship
- Enhanced screening — Cygnus Scan PEP database check
- Expected transaction profile — is this a one-time purchase or will there be more?
- Semi-annual review scheduled from the start

**Step 5: Set review frequency.** High risk = every 6 months.

**Step 6: Escalate to Compliance Officer.** All PEP relationships are escalated for CO review. The CO will determine whether to proceed, request additional information, or decline.

**Key point:** Three EDD triggers are present simultaneously: non-resident, PEP family member, and high-value. When multiple triggers converge, the scrutiny increases proportionately.

## Scenario 4: The Corporate Client with Red Flags

**Client:** Azure Capital Partners Ltd, a DIFC-registered consultancy firm. The sole shareholder is a Russian national who is a UAE resident. The firm wants to purchase a AED 15 million commercial property. The representative mentions payment will come from multiple accounts in different countries.

**Step 1: Risk classification.** Multiple high-risk factors. Consultancy firm — high-risk entity type. Russian national shareholder — high-risk under Prime Capital's individual table. DIFC free zone — while DIFC is well-regulated, free zone companies are classified as high-risk. Corporate client — EDD required for all corporate purchases. Overall: high-risk on multiple dimensions.

**Step 2: Identify additional risk factors.**
- Payment from multiple accounts in different countries — transaction red flag (complicated routing without explanation)
- Russian national — classified as high-risk under Prime Capital's individual table
- Sole shareholder — clear UBO identification, but the individual's risk profile drives the entity's risk

**Step 3: Determine CDD level.** Enhanced Due Diligence — mandatory for corporate clients, Russian national ownership, and free zone entities. The multi-country payment arrangement adds an additional layer of scrutiny.

**Step 4: Apply EDD measures.**
- All 9 standard CDD measures
- Complete corporate KYC with 12 documents
- UBO identification — the sole Russian national shareholder is the clear UBO
- Detailed source of funds inquiry — which accounts, which countries, and why multiple accounts? What is the business rationale for the payment structure?
- Source of wealth for the UBO — how was the wealth accumulated?
- Senior management approval required
- Enhanced monitoring from the start
- Specific inquiry into the multi-country payment arrangement — is there a legitimate business reason?

**Step 5: Set review frequency.** High risk = every 6 months.

**Step 6: Escalate to Compliance Officer.** The multi-country payment arrangement is a specific red flag that the CO needs to assess. The CO may request additional documentation, impose conditions on the payment structure (e.g., all funds must come from a single verifiable account), or decline the relationship.

**Key point:** This scenario shows how red flags and risk factors compound. Any one factor might be manageable. Multiple factors together require your highest level of scrutiny and immediate CO escalation.

## Summary Decision Framework

When a new client approaches:

1. **Check nationality** — is it a FATF grey/blacklist country? Is it Iran or Russia (high-risk at Prime Capital)?
2. **Check residency** — are they a UAE resident or non-resident?
3. **Check entity type** — if corporate, what category?
4. **Check for PEP** — are they or their family/associates politically exposed?
5. **Check payment method** — cash, single transfer, or complex multi-source?
6. **Assign risk level** — using the risk rating tables
7. **Apply corresponding CDD level** — SDD, standard CDD, or EDD
8. **Set review frequency** — 2 years, 1 year, or 6 months
9. **Escalate if needed** — to the Compliance Officer for any EDD case or red flags

The more risk factors that converge, the higher the scrutiny. When in doubt, escalate.
