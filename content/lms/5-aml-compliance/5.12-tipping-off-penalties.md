# Tipping Off, Cash Policy, Record Keeping & Penalties

## Introduction

This final module covers four essential topics: the tipping off prohibition (which carries criminal penalties), Prime Capital's cash acceptance policy, the record-keeping requirements that support all AML compliance, and the penalty framework that applies to violations. These are the rules that protect you — and the consequences when they are broken.

## Why This Matters

Tipping off is one of the most serious compliance violations because it actively undermines investigations. Cash policies exist because cash transactions are the primary entry point for illicit funds. Record-keeping ensures that everything can be traced and verified. And the penalty framework makes the consequences of non-compliance unmistakably clear.

---

## Tipping Off (Policy s.18)

### Definition

**Tipping off is a federal crime.** It occurs when any person informs a client or any third party that:

- A suspicious transaction report **has been filed**
- A suspicious transaction report **will be filed**
- An investigation **is underway** or is being considered

### Who It Applies To

The prohibition applies to **all employees, senior managers, officials, and staff** — directly or indirectly. You cannot:

- Tell the client directly ("We have reported you")
- Tell a colleague who might tell the client
- Hint or imply that an investigation is happening
- Change your behaviour in a way that alerts the client
- Discuss STR details with anyone outside the compliance function

### Penalties

| Penalty | Amount/Duration |
|---------|----------------|
| **Fines** | AED 100,000 to AED 500,000 |
| **Imprisonment** | Minimum 6 months |
| **Employment** | Termination from Prime Capital |

**These penalties are cumulative** — you can face both the fine and imprisonment, plus termination.

### Company Obligations

Prime Capital must:

- Maintain **strict confidentiality** of STR content and the act of reporting
- Implement **system controls** — access restrictions, secure communication channels
- **Train staff** on tipping off prohibitions and consequences
- Ensure that **all suspicious transactions** are kept fully confidential

### When CDD Would Tip Off

A special situation arises when performing CDD itself would alert the customer to an investigation:

- If CDD measures would **tip off the customer** about a suspected investigation → **Do NOT apply CDD**
- Instead, **notify the FIU immediately** via an STR/SAR
- Include in the report the **reasons CDD was not performed**
- The FIU will provide guidance on next steps

This is an exception to the CDD obligation — and it only applies when performing CDD would compromise an investigation.

### Practical Guidance

**What you can say:**
- "We are processing your documentation" (normal business language)
- "There is a delay in our compliance review" (factual, non-specific)

**What you must NEVER say:**
- "We have filed a report about your transaction"
- "Your account has been flagged"
- "There is an investigation into your funds"
- "I cannot tell you why, but you should be careful"
- Anything that would cause the client to suspect they are under scrutiny

## Cash Acceptance Policy (Policy s.19)

### Prime Capital's Position

Prime Capital **discourages accepting cash** above AED 55,000 per transaction.

### Approved Payment Methods

| Method | Status |
|--------|--------|
| **Bank transfers** | Standard and encouraged |
| **Cheques** | Standard and encouraged |
| **Cash below AED 55,000** | Accepted with standard CDD |
| **Cash AED 55,000 or above** | Discouraged — must use traceable methods |

### Above the Threshold

When cash equals or exceeds AED 55,000 (single transaction or linked transactions):

1. **Traceable payment methods must be used** — redirect to bank transfer or cheque
2. **Comprehensive KYC and CDD** must be applied
3. **EDD** is required for high-risk cases (PEPs, unusual patterns)
4. **REAR reporting** is triggered — the transaction must be reported to the FIU via goAML

### In Practice

The vast majority of property transactions in Dubai are conducted via bank transfer. Cash transactions at the AED 55,000+ level are unusual and should be treated with heightened scrutiny. If a client insists on paying a significant amount in cash:

- Explain that UAE regulations require traceable payment methods for large transactions
- Offer to facilitate a bank transfer instead
- If the client insists on cash, escalate to the Compliance Officer before proceeding
- Document the interaction regardless of outcome

## Record Keeping (Policy s.20)

### What Must Be Retained

Prime Capital must maintain accurate and complete records of:

**CDD Documents:**
- Client identification documents (passport, EID copies)
- Beneficial ownership details
- Source of funds documentation
- Risk assessments
- Screening results

**Transaction Records:**
- Contracts (sales agreements, LOIs, MOUs)
- Title deeds
- Payment evidence (bank transfer confirmations, receipts)
- Commission agreements

**Internal Compliance Records:**
- STRs filed to the FIU
- ISTRs filed internally
- Compliance review reports
- Staff training logs

**AML/CFT Programme Documentation:**
- Policies and procedures
- Compliance reviews and audit reports
- Correspondence with authorities

### Retention Period

**Minimum 5 years** from:
- The **end of the business relationship**, OR
- The **date of the transaction** (whichever is later)

### Accessibility and Security

- Records must be **kept securely** — protected from unauthorised access, tampering, or destruction
- Records must remain **accessible** — available for inspection by regulators, auditors, and law enforcement upon request
- Electronic records are acceptable provided they meet security and accessibility standards

### Why This Matters

Proper record-keeping supports:
- **Transparency** — clear audit trail for all transactions
- **Investigations** — evidence available if questions arise
- **Mitigating legal/regulatory risks** — documentation is your defence in case of disputes or audits
- **Continuity** — if you leave the firm, your records protect the firm and the clients you worked with

## Penalty Framework (Policy s.25)

### Penalties for Money Laundering — Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018

| Article | Violation | Penalty |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **22(1)** | ML acts under Article 2 | Imprisonment up to 10 years AND fine AED 100,000 to AED 5,000,000 |
| **22(1) Aggravated** | ML through abuse of professional position, NPO, organised group, or recidivism | Fine AED 300,000 to AED 10,000,000 |
| **22(2)** | Attempted ML | Full penalty prescribed for completed offence |
| **22(3)** | Using proceeds for terrorist financing | Life imprisonment or 10+ years AND fine AED 300,000 to AED 10,000,000 |
| **22(4)** | Using proceeds to finance illegal organisations | Imprisonment + fine ≥ AED 300,000 |
| **22(5)** | Providing information leading to disclosure/prosecution | Court may reduce or exempt sentence |
| **23(1)** | ML by legal persons (companies) | Fine AED 500,000 to AED 50,000,000 |
| **23(2)** | TF conviction of legal person | **Dissolution and closure** of the company |
| **24** | Failure to report (Article 15) | Imprisonment + fine AED 100,000 to AED 1,000,000 |
| **25** | Tipping off | Imprisonment ≥ 6 months + fine AED 100,000 to AED 500,000 |
| **25 bis** | Possessing/concealing funds with evidence of illegality | Imprisonment ≥ 3 months + fine ≥ AED 50,000 |
| **26 bis** | Violating Article 16 bis | Imprisonment ≥ 6 months + fine AED 200,000 to AED 5,000,000 |
| **28** | Violating TFS directives | Imprisonment + fine AED 50,000 to AED 5,000,000 |
| **31** | Any other violation | Imprisonment + fine AED 10,000 to AED 100,000 |

### Key Points

- **Attempted ML carries the same penalty** as completed ML — you do not need to succeed to be prosecuted
- **Companies can be fined up to AED 50 million** — and dissolved if convicted of TF
- **Failure to report is a standalone crime** — even if no ML actually occurred, not reporting suspicion is punishable
- **Tipping off carries minimum 6 months** — there is no suspended sentence or fine-only option
- **Aggravated factors** include abuse of professional position — as a licensed broker, this directly applies to you
- **Cooperation with authorities** (Article 22(5)) may reduce or exempt penalties — another reason to report

### Company Disciplinary Action

In addition to legal penalties, Prime Capital may take disciplinary action including:
- Formal warning
- Suspension
- **Termination** of employment

## Key Takeaways

1. **Tipping off is a federal crime** — minimum 6 months imprisonment + fines AED 100K-500K
2. **Never inform a client** that they are under suspicion, have been reported, or are being investigated
3. **Cash ≥ AED 55,000** — discouraged; must use traceable methods; triggers REAR reporting
4. **5-year minimum retention** for all CDD, transaction, and compliance records
5. **ML penalties reach** imprisonment up to 10 years + fines up to AED 5M for individuals, AED 50M for companies
6. **Failure to report** is a standalone crime — imprisonment + fines AED 100K-1M
7. **Attempted ML** carries the same penalty as completed ML
8. **TF conviction of a company** means dissolution and closure — the ultimate corporate penalty
