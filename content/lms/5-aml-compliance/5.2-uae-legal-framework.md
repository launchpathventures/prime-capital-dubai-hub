# UAE Legal & Regulatory Framework

## Introduction

The UAE has built a comprehensive legal framework for combating money laundering, terrorist financing, and proliferation financing. This framework consists of federal laws, cabinet decisions, circulars, and supplemental guidance — all of which apply to you as a real estate professional.

This module maps the regulatory landscape so you understand which laws govern your conduct, which authorities oversee compliance, and what specific obligations you carry as a DNFBP.

## Why This Matters

Ignorance of the law is not a defence. When RERA or the Ministry of Economy audits your compliance, they will assess you against these specific laws and regulations. Understanding the framework is not academic — it is the basis on which you will be evaluated and, if necessary, penalised.

---

## Primary Legislation

### Federal Decree-Law No. (20) of 2018

This is the **primary AML/CFT legislation** in the UAE — commonly referred to as "the Decree Law." It establishes:

- Definitions of ML, TF, and PF offences
- Obligations for reporting entities (including DNFBPs)
- Penalties for violations (Articles 22–31)
- Powers of the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)
- Requirements for customer due diligence and record keeping

**This is the law you must know.** All other regulations, decisions, and circulars derive from or supplement this decree.

### Federal Decree-Law No. (10) of 2025

Updated and refined definitions of ML offences. Article 2 provides the six acts that constitute money laundering — the definitions covered in Module 5.1.

### Federal Decree No. (26) of 2021

Amendments to the primary AML/CFT framework, strengthening enforcement mechanisms and expanding the scope of obligations.

---

## Implementing Regulations

### Cabinet Decision No. (10) of 2019

The implementing regulations for the Decree Law. This decision:

- Specifies **how** the obligations in the Decree Law must be carried out
- **Article 3** classifies real estate agents and brokers as DNFBPs — this is the legal basis for your obligations
- Sets out CDD requirements, record-keeping standards, and reporting procedures
- Defines the risk-based approach to compliance

### Cabinet Decision No. (74) of 2020

Governs terrorism lists and targeted financial sanctions (TFS):

- Procedures for implementing UN Security Council sanctions
- Requirements for freezing assets of designated persons
- Reporting obligations for confirmed and partial name matches
- Framework for the UAE Local Terrorist List

### Cabinet Decision No. (109) of 2023

Updates procedures for identifying Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs):

- Defines the 25% ownership/voting rights threshold
- Specifies the cascade of identification methods when no natural person meets the threshold
- Requirements for maintaining UBO registers

### Cabinet Resolution No. (71) of 2024

Establishes the framework for administrative penalties:

- Categories of violations and corresponding penalties
- Penalty ranges for non-compliance
- Procedures for appeals and dispute resolution

These circulars provide specific, current guidance that supplements the legislation:

| Circular | Subject | Key Content |

### Supplemental Guidance for Real Estate Sector

The Ministry of Economy has issued specific guidance for the real estate sector covering:

- Application of CDD requirements to property transactions
- Red flag indicators specific to real estate
- Record-keeping requirements for brokers and agents
- Reporting obligations and timelines

## Regulatory Authorities

### Ministry of Economy

The **supervisory authority for DNFBPs** in the UAE. The Ministry of Economy:

- Oversees compliance by real estate agents, brokers, and other DNFBPs
- Conducts inspections and audits
- Issues circulars and guidance
- Imposes administrative penalties for non-compliance

**For you:** The Ministry of Economy is the authority that can audit your AML compliance. Their inspectors can request your KYC records, CDD documentation, and training logs at any time.

### Executive Office of Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing (EOCN)

The EOCN coordinates national AML/CFT efforts:

- Manages the **Notification Alert System (NAS)** — automated sanctions list updates
- Coordinates implementation of targeted financial sanctions
- Issues freezing directives
- Communicates with UN Sanctions Committee

**For you:** Prime Capital is registered with the EOCN NAS system, which sends automated alerts when sanctions lists are updated. You will learn more about this in Module 5.9.

### Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)

The FIU receives and analyses suspicious transaction reports:

- Receives STR/SAR, CNMR, PNMR, and REAR reports via the **goAML** platform
- Analyses reports and disseminates intelligence to law enforcement
- Can request additional information from reporting entities
- Coordinates with international FIUs

**For you:** The goAML platform is where Prime Capital files all external reports. You will learn the reporting procedures in Module 5.11.

### Central Bank AML/CFT Supervision

While the Central Bank primarily supervises financial institutions (banks, exchange houses), it plays a role in the broader AML/CFT ecosystem:

- Sets standards for financial institutions that handle property transaction funds
- Issues guidance on payment methods and fund transfers
- Coordinates with the Ministry of Economy on cross-sector issues

## Real Estate Agents as DNFBPs

### What is a DNFBP?

A Designated Non-Financial Business or Profession is a category of business that, while not a financial institution, handles transactions that can be exploited for ML/TF. Under **Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019, Article 3**, DNFBPs include:

- **Real estate agents and brokers** (this is you)
- Dealers in precious metals and stones
- Lawyers, notaries, and legal professionals
- Accountants and auditors
- Corporate service providers
- Trust service providers

### Why Are Real Estate Agents DNFBPs?

Because you:

- **Facilitate high-value transactions** — property purchases involve large sums
- **Interact directly with buyers** — you are often the first point of contact
- **Collect client information** — you are in a position to identify suspicious activity
- **Handle transaction documentation** — you process contracts and payment instructions

### The 8 Core AML/CFT Obligations for DNFBPs

As a DNFBP, you have **eight specific obligations** under UAE law:

1. **Customer Due Diligence (CDD)** — Verify the identity of all clients and beneficial owners before any transaction
2. **Risk Assessment** — Assess ML/TF/PF risks at enterprise, product, and customer levels
3. **Record Keeping** — Maintain all CDD and transaction records for a minimum of 5 years
4. **Suspicious Transaction Reporting** — Report suspicious activity to the FIU via goAML
5. **Targeted Financial Sanctions** — Screen against sanctions lists and implement TFS measures
6. **Internal Controls** — Establish policies, procedures, and controls to prevent ML/TF
7. **Training** — Complete regular AML/CFT training and awareness programmes
8. **Compliance Programme** — Maintain a documented AML/CFT compliance programme with a designated Compliance Officer

**Every single obligation applies to you.** There are no exemptions for junior agents, part-time staff, or new hires. From day one, you are legally responsible.

## FATF — The International Framework

### What is FATF?

The Financial Action Task Force is the global standard-setter for AML/CFT measures. Established in 1989, FATF:

- Issues the **40 Recommendations** — the international AML/CFT standards
- Conducts mutual evaluations of member countries
- Maintains lists of high-risk and monitored jurisdictions
- Promotes effective implementation of legal, regulatory, and operational measures

### FATF's 7 Key Themes

The 40 Recommendations are organised around 7 themes:

1. **AML/CFT policies and coordination** — National frameworks and inter-agency cooperation
2. **Money laundering and confiscation** — Criminalisation and asset recovery
3. **Terrorist financing and proliferation financing** — Specific offences and targeted sanctions
4. **Preventive measures** — CDD, record-keeping, reporting
5. **Transparency of legal persons and arrangements** — Beneficial ownership
6. **Powers and responsibilities of competent authorities** — Supervisory and enforcement powers
7. **International cooperation** — Mutual legal assistance and information sharing

### FATF Grey List vs Black List

FATF maintains two lists that directly affect your work:

**Grey List (Jurisdictions Under Increased Monitoring):**
Countries with strategic deficiencies in their AML/CFT frameworks that have committed to action plans. As of June 2025 (per Prime Capital's Policy Appendix II):

Algeria, Angola, Bulgaria, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cote d'Ivoire, Croatia, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Haiti, Kenya, Lebanon, Mali, Monaco, Mozambique, Namibia, Nepal, Nigeria, Philippines, South Africa, South Sudan, Syria, Tanzania, Venezuela, Vietnam, Yemen

**Black List (High-Risk Jurisdictions Subject to a Call for Action):**
Countries with significant strategic deficiencies. FATF calls on all members to apply enhanced due diligence:

Iran, Myanmar, North Korea (DPRK)

**What this means for you:**
- Clients from **grey list** countries require enhanced scrutiny and potentially EDD
- Clients from **black list** countries are either **prohibited** (North Korea, Myanmar nationals are prohibited at Prime Capital) or require the highest level of EDD (Iran nationals are classified as high-risk)
- You must know which countries are on these lists — they affect your risk assessment for every international client

## Key Takeaways

1. **Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018** is the primary AML/CFT law — know it
2. **Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019, Article 3** classifies you as a DNFBP — this is the legal basis for your obligations
3. **You have 8 specific obligations** as a DNFBP — CDD, risk assessment, record keeping, reporting, TFS, internal controls, training, and compliance programme
4. **Three key authorities** oversee your compliance: Ministry of Economy (supervisory), EOCN (sanctions/TFS), and FIU (reporting via goAML)
5. **FATF grey and black lists** directly affect how you assess client risk — know the current countries
6. **Multiple laws apply simultaneously** — the Decree Law, cabinet decisions, and circulars all form part of your obligations
