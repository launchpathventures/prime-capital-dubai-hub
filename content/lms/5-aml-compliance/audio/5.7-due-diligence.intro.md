Welcome to Module 5.7. This module connects risk assessment to action. Once you have rated a client's risk, you must apply the corresponding level of due diligence. Low risk may qualify for Simplified Due Diligence. Medium risk gets standard CDD. High risk gets Enhanced Due Diligence — on top of standard CDD, not instead of it.

The critical skill here is knowing which level to apply and when to escalate. A government entity normally qualifies for SDD — but if anything suspicious surfaces, SDD is immediately off the table. A non-resident automatically triggers EDD. A PEP triggers EDD plus senior management approval.

You will learn the 9 specific CDD measures that Prime Capital implements, the complete list of EDD triggers, and the review frequencies that apply to each risk level — two years for low risk, one year for medium, six months for high.

You will also learn what happens when CDD cannot be completed. If the client refuses to provide documents or you cannot verify their identity, you cannot proceed. No exceptions.

Due diligence is not optional, not flexible, and not negotiable. The level must match the risk.
