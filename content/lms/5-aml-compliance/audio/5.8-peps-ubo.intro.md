Welcome to Module 5.8. Two categories of clients demand your sharpest attention: Politically Exposed Persons and transactions involving complex beneficial ownership structures.

PEPs are not inherently criminal. Many are legitimate investors with legitimate wealth. But their access to public funds and political influence makes them inherently higher risk for corruption and money laundering. And critically, PEP status extends beyond the individual — their spouses, children, parents, siblings, and close associates are all treated as PEPs too.

In this module, you will learn exactly who qualifies as a PEP under Prime Capital's framework, the three categories we screen for, and the mandatory procedures when a PEP is identified — including the requirement for senior management approval before establishing the relationship. There are no exceptions to this requirement.

You will also deepen your understanding of UBO identification. The 25 percent threshold is your starting point. But when no single person meets the threshold, you need to know the cascade: aggregate ownership, then control through other means, then the highest-ranking management official.

Former PEPs remain high-risk. PEP status does not expire when the political role ends. Keep that in mind.
