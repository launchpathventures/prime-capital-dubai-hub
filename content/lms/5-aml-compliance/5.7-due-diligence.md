# Customer Due Diligence — SDD, CDD & EDD

## Introduction

Customer Due Diligence is the practical implementation of the risk-based approach. Based on the risk rating you assigned in Module 5.4, you apply one of three levels of due diligence: Simplified (SDD), Standard (CDD), or Enhanced (EDD). Getting the level right is critical — too little scrutiny on a high-risk client is a compliance failure; too much on a low-risk client wastes resources and damages the client experience.

This module covers when each level applies, what measures each involves, and the specific triggers that require escalation from CDD to EDD.

## Why This Matters

Due diligence is where your risk assessment becomes action. A risk rating without corresponding due diligence is meaningless — and vice versa. The level of CDD you apply must be proportionate to the risk, documented properly, and reviewed at the correct frequency.

---

## The Risk-Based CDD Framework

| Risk Level | CDD Level | When to Apply |
|------------|-----------|---------------|
| **Low** | Simplified Due Diligence (SDD) | Only for specific low-risk categories with no suspicion |
| **Medium** | Standard CDD | Default level for most clients |
| **High** | Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) | Applied IN ADDITION to standard CDD |

**Critical rule:** EDD does not replace CDD — it is applied on top of it. A high-risk client receives all standard CDD measures PLUS additional enhanced measures.

---

## Simplified Due Diligence (SDD)

### When SDD May Apply

SDD is the lowest level of due diligence. It may **only** be applied when ALL of the following conditions are met:

1. The client is a **public company listed on a stock exchange** with adequate disclosure requirements, OR
2. The client is a **government or semi-government entity**, AND
3. The firm's risk assessment confirms **low risk** for both the customer and the transaction

### SDD Measures

When SDD applies, you may:

- Verify identity **after** establishing the business relationship (rather than before)
- Reduce the frequency of identity updates
- Reduce the frequency of ongoing monitoring
- Infer the purpose of the transaction from the transaction type

### Absolute Prohibition

**Never apply SDD when there is any suspicion of ML/TF** — regardless of the client's risk classification. The moment suspicion arises, SDD is no longer appropriate and standard CDD (or EDD) must be applied immediately.

**In practice:** SDD is rarely used at Prime Capital. Most clients require standard CDD or EDD.

---

## Standard Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

### When CDD is Required

CDD must be performed when any of the following occur:

1. **Any transaction** on the customer's name or behalf
2. **Buying, selling, or leasing** property
3. **Giving advice**, information, or analysis
4. **Signing any contract**, agreement, LOI, or MOU
5. **Accepting any compensation or deposit**
6. **Receiving funds/proceeds** from or on behalf of a customer
7. **Occasional transactions** ≥ AED 55,000 (single or linked)
8. **Suspicion of crime** — regardless of transaction amount
9. **Doubts about previously obtained** identification data

**In plain terms:** CDD is required before you do almost anything with a client. If in doubt, apply CDD.

### The 9 CDD Measures (Policy s.9.2.3)

Prime Capital's CDD involves 9 specific measures:

1. **Obtain legally valid documents** — For foreign nationals and beneficial owners, collect documents legally valid in the issuing jurisdiction
2. **Identify and verify** — Establish the identity of the customer, beneficial owners, and beneficiaries from reliable, independent sources
3. **Background screening** — Screen against sanctions lists, international watch lists, and adverse media databases (using Cygnus Scan)
4. **Determine purpose** — Understand the purpose of the business relationship and the legal entity structure (if corporate)
5. **Continuously monitor** — Monitor transactions against the client's risk profile throughout the relationship
6. **Scrutinise transactions** — Examine transactions for consistency with your knowledge of the customer, their business, and risk profile
7. **Keep records current** — Maintain up-to-date CDD records, especially for high-risk clients
8. **Exercise enhanced levels** — For higher-risk situations, verify source of funds and apply additional measures
9. **Verify from originals** — Use original, government-issued identification documents for verification

### CDD Timing Exception

In limited circumstances, you may verify identity **after** establishing the business relationship, but ONLY if:

- There is **no suspicion** of ML/TF
- The ML/TF risk is assessed as **low**
- Funds are **held in escrow** until verification is complete
- Verification is a **prerequisite** to closing the transaction

This exception exists to facilitate legitimate transactions where delays in verification would cause unnecessary disruption — but it does not reduce your obligations.

### Authorised Representatives

When a client acts through an authorised representative (POA holder, authorised signatory):

- The **same CDD applies** to the representative as would apply to the client
- The representative must hold a **valid authorising document** (POA, board resolution, etc.)
- Collect identification for **both** the client and the representative
- Verify the **authenticity and scope** of the authorising document

## Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

### When EDD is Required

EDD must be applied for ALL of the following categories:

1. **UAE non-residents** — Any client who does not reside in the UAE
2. **High-value cash transactions** — Cash amounts above the reporting threshold
3. **Suspicious transactions** — Any transaction where indicators of ML/TF are present
4. **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)** — PEPs and their family members/close associates
5. **Corporate customers** — All legal entity clients
6. **Customers from high-risk countries** — Nationals of FATF grey-list or black-list countries
7. **Dealers in real estate and precious metals** — Clients in these high-risk sectors
8. **Other cases** — Any situation where the Compliance Officer determines EDD is appropriate

### EDD Measures (Policy s.9.7)

Enhanced Due Diligence involves ALL standard CDD measures PLUS:

- **Increased scrutiny** and higher verification standards for all documentation
- **Detailed inquiry** into the purpose of the relationship, nature of the client's business, and source of funds
- **Independent evidence** of source of funds — not just client declarations, but bank statements and third-party verification
- **Expected transaction profile** — Collect information on expected transaction types, volumes, locations, and timing
- **Senior management approval** — Required before establishing the business relationship
- **Semi-annual updates** — Update customer and beneficial owner information every 6 months minimum
- **Enhanced monitoring** — Increased frequency of transaction monitoring, more controls, and pattern analysis

### 8 Specific High-Risk Factors Requiring EDD

1. Customer from a **FATF high-risk jurisdiction** or non-resident without verifiable ID
2. **PEP** or foreign PEP (individual or beneficial owner)
3. Legal entity with **complex operations** or unclear economic objectives
4. Legal entity with **complex ownership structure**
5. Business relationship or transactions involving **high-risk jurisdictions**
6. **Cash-intensive** legal entity
7. **NPOs** (Non-Profit Organisations)
8. Any other person identified as **high-risk through customer risk assessment**

### 4 EDD Risk Factor Categories

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Geography risk** | Transactions with FATF grey/blacklist countries |
| **Product/services/channel risk** | High-value cash, complex ownership, third-party/proxy transactions |
| **Transaction risk** | Cash payments, high-value deals, crypto/virtual assets, international transfers |
| **Customer type risk** | Non-residents, high-risk businesses, NPOs, PEPs |

## CDD Review Frequencies

| Risk Level | Review Frequency | What to Review |
|------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Low** | Every **2 years** | ID validity, contact details, risk classification |
| **Medium** | Every **1 year** | All low-risk items plus source of funds, screening, transaction patterns |
| **High** | Every **6 months** | All items above plus enhanced monitoring results, source of wealth verification, updated screening |

### Trigger-Based Reviews

In addition to scheduled reviews, CDD must be updated whenever:

- There is a **significant change** in the client's circumstances (new job, moved country, changed business)
- A **suspicious indicator** is identified
- The client's **risk profile changes** (e.g., they become a PEP)
- **New adverse media** or screening results emerge
- **CDD measures would tip off** the client about an investigation (in this case, do NOT perform CDD — instead file an STR and notify the Compliance Officer)

---

## When CDD Cannot Be Completed

If you cannot complete CDD satisfactorily — the client refuses to provide documents, you cannot verify identity, or screening raises unresolvable concerns:

1. **Do not proceed** with the transaction
2. **Do not establish** the business relationship
3. **Consider filing an ISTR** — the inability to complete CDD may itself indicate suspicious activity
4. **Document everything** — record what was attempted, what was missing, and why CDD could not be completed
5. **Notify the Compliance Officer**

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **SDD is rare** — only for government entities, listed companies, and confirmed low-risk situations with no suspicion
2. **CDD is the default** — required before almost any business activity with a client
3. **EDD is additive** — it supplements CDD, it does not replace it
4. **9 CDD measures** define what standard due diligence involves at Prime Capital
5. **Senior management approval** is required for EDD relationships
6. **Review frequencies are mandatory** — Low = 2 years, Medium = 1 year, High = 6 months
7. **When CDD cannot be completed** — do not proceed, document, escalate, consider ISTR
