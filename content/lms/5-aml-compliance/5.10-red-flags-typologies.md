# Red Flags & Real Estate ML/FT Typologies

## Introduction

Red flags are indicators — warning signs that a transaction or client may involve money laundering, terrorist financing, or other financial crime. They are not proof of criminal activity. But they are signals that require further investigation, enhanced due diligence, or escalation to the Compliance Officer.

This module covers Prime Capital's comprehensive list of red flag indicators (drawn from the AML/CFT Policy Appendix I) and 10 real estate ML/FT typology case studies (from Appendix III). Together, these give you the pattern-recognition skills to spot suspicious activity in your daily work.

## Why This Matters

The difference between a compliant consultant and a non-compliant one is often the ability to recognise red flags. You will encounter legitimate clients who trigger one or two flags — that is normal. But a client who triggers multiple flags simultaneously warrants serious attention. Your ability to recognise, document, and escalate these patterns is your primary contribution to the firm's AML defence.

---

## Natural Person Red Flags

These indicators relate to individual clients. Any single flag warrants attention; multiple flags require escalation.

### Behavioural Indicators
- **Reluctant to provide personal information** or explain business activities, beneficial ownership, source of wealth/funds, reason for activities, transaction partners, or third-party dealings
- **Interested in foreign company formation** in low-tax or secrecy jurisdictions without commercial explanation
- **Conducting transactions incompatible** with their socio-economic or professional profile
- **Signatory to multiple companies** without explanation
- **Unusual interest in facilitating** another party's business arrangements
- **Insists on intermediary** for all interactions without justification
- **Actively avoids personal contact**
- **Foreign national with no significant dealings** in the country, no clear economic rationale for real estate transaction
- **Refuses to cooperate** or provide information/documents
- **Makes unusual requests** — especially relating to secrecy
- **PEP status** or familial/professional associations with a PEP

---

## Legal Person/Arrangement Red Flags

These indicators relate to corporate clients, trusts, and other legal arrangements.

### Entity Verification Indicators
- **Cannot demonstrate real business activity** or operating history
- **Not findable on internet or LinkedIn** — no online presence
- **Directors/shareholders/BOs appear as representatives** of other legal persons (professional nominees)
- **Registered under a name** that does not match its declared business activity
- **Name appears to mimic** high-profile multinational corporations
- **Uses public/non-professional email domain** (Gmail, Hotmail, Yahoo) for corporate communications
- **Registered at address** that does not match its profile or is not locatable on maps
- **Incorporated in a high ML/TF risk jurisdiction**
- **Incorporated in a jurisdiction** where BO reporting is not required
- **Unnecessarily complex corporate structure** without business justification
- **Unusually large number** of beneficiaries, controlling interests, or signatories
- **Registered at a shared address** (mailbox service indicator)
- **Directors/shareholders cannot be located or contacted**

## Transaction Red Flags

These indicators relate to the transaction itself — the purchase, sale, or lease.

### Relationship and Rationale
- **Questionable connections** between transaction parties
- **No apparent business or trade rationale** for the transaction
- **Family member involvement** without legitimate reason
- **Repeat transactions between the same parties**
- **Third-party financing** without logical explanation
- **Loans from private third parties** without documentation

### Financial Indicators
- **Funds from an entity going into liquidation or receivership**
- **Business account used for personal purchases**
- **Complicated routing** of funds without explanation
- **Transfer of real property** from natural person to legal entity off-market
- **Multiple large cash payments** on a loan or mortgage
- **Early loan/mortgage repayment** — especially with prepayment penalties accepted
- **Involves persons linked to criminal activities**
- **Several linked transactions** (same parties, nationality, address, or representatives)

### Buyer Behaviour
- **Not interested in better price or payment terms** — accepts above-market pricing without negotiation
- **Strong interest in completing quickly** without good cause
- **Interest in specific area** without caring about price
- **Foreign or non-resident for tax purposes**
- **Capital investment only** — no intention to live in or lease the property
- **Large-scale operations** — buying large plots, complete buildings
- **Intermediaries acting for groups** of associated individuals/entities
- **Unusual contractual terms**
- **No particular interest in property characteristics** (quality, construction, handover date)

### Geographic and Source Indicators
- **Funds from or to a foreign country** with no apparent connection to the client
- **Funds from low-tax or offshore jurisdiction**
- **Recently created legal entities** with large amounts relative to assets
- **Cash collateral used for loan** shortly after property purchase
- **Unexplained use of POA** or delegation processes
- **Persons in tax havens or high-risk countries** with matching transaction characteristics
- **Minors or incapacitated persons** as transaction parties
- **Foundations/cultural/leisure/NPOs** with transaction goals mismatched to their stated purpose

### Ownership and Structure
- **Companies mainly owned by foreign nationals** (may not be resident for tax)
- **Real estate contributed to a company** with no registered address or permanent establishment
- **Parties acting on behalf of a hidden real customer**
- **Unexplained last-minute identity or ownership changes**
- **Intermediaries who are foreign or non-resident** for tax purposes
- **Unnecessarily complex transactions** making BO identification difficult
- **Numbers, sizes, or types** inconsistent with expected activity
- **Far larger than declared income or turnover**
- **Changes in ownership** of legal entities or real estate with unclear rationale and/or third-party involvement with unclear source of funds
- **Payments from third parties** whose relationship to the buyer is unclear
- **Business structures involving high-risk countries** without clear reasons
- **Cannot explain source of funds** or explanation lacks credibility
- **CDD measures cannot be performed** or customer provides false, misleading, or forged documentation
- **Large cash amounts** without adequate explanation

---

## Means of Payment Red Flags

- **Cash or negotiable instruments** that do not state the true payer
- **Amount of such instruments** is significant relative to transaction value
- **Payments divided into smaller parts** with short intervals (structuring/smurfing)
- **Doubts about validity** of loan application documents
- **Loan granted or attempted** using cash collateral deposited abroad

---

## Other Red Flags

- **Contract cancellation penalising the buyer** (loss of deposit if sale does not proceed)
- **Subsequent additional transactions on the same property** in rapid succession with significant price changes
- **Agreed value significantly different from market value**
- **Property development in a high-risk area** (economic or environmental)
- **Recording sale of building plot followed by completed building** in unrealistically short time
- **Private contract with no intention to notarise or register**

## 10 Real Estate ML/FT Typology Case Studies

These case studies from Prime Capital's Policy (Appendix III) illustrate real patterns of money laundering through real estate. Study them for pattern recognition.

### Typology 1: Concealing Illicit Funds Through Real Estate
**Pattern:** Tax fraud proceeds → real estate purchases to disguise origin.
**Case:** Individual commits tax fraud in Hungary, uses proceeds to purchase real estate, claiming legitimate investment income.
**Red flags:** Source of funds inconsistent with declared income, rapid property accumulation.

### Typology 2: Cash Transport, Exchange Houses, and Real Estate
**Pattern:** Drug proceeds → exchange houses → real estate.
**Case:** Drug trafficking proceeds in Croatia are physically transported to exchange houses, converted, and used to purchase property.
**Red flags:** Cash-intensive origin, use of exchange houses, geographic mismatch between client and property.

### Typology 3: Smurfing / Structuring
**Pattern:** Large illicit sum → broken into smaller amounts → property purchase.
**Case:** A US attorney structures $177,500 in cash using hockey players as intermediaries, purchasing cashier's cheques in amounts below reporting thresholds to buy property.
**Red flags:** Multiple small deposits or payments, use of cashier's cheques, third-party intermediaries.

### Typology 4: Successive Transactions for ML Purposes
**Pattern:** Shell companies + bearer shares → land purchase → reclassification → profit.
**Case:** A lawyer creates shell companies with bearer shares, purchases agricultural land, has it reclassified as residential, and sells at profit — laundering the proceeds through complex corporate layers.
**Red flags:** Bearer shares, shell companies, rapid reclassification, professional facilitator.

### Typology 5: Cross-Border Transfers via Spouse
**Pattern:** Criminal income → spouse's account → cross-border → property.
**Case:** Asian national purchases restaurant using mortgage, repaid from spouse's account receiving cross-border transfers from China. Connected to human smuggling network.
**Red flags:** Cross-border payments inconsistent with business, spouse used as conduit, rapid mortgage repayment.

### Typology 6: Real Estate Agent as Bank Introduction
**Pattern:** Trust structure → real estate agent → bank account → TF.
**Case:** Individual uses an offshore trust to purchase property in Belgium. The real estate agent introduces the buyer to a bank, which opens an account. The account is later used for terrorist financing.
**Red flags:** Offshore trust, agent facilitating bank introduction, no clear rationale for account.

### Typology 7: Family Members as Fronts
**Pattern:** Criminal → parents' names → property purchase → rapid mortgage repayment.
**Case:** Canadian with criminal connections purchases properties in parents' names. Mortgages are repaid within 6 months using unexplained funds.
**Red flags:** Family nominees, rapid mortgage repayment, source of funds inconsistent with parents' profile.

### Typology 8: Exploitation of Charity
**Pattern:** Cash deposits → charity → property → STR.
**Case:** Individual A makes cash deposits, uses power of attorney to channel funds through a charity, which purchases property. Bank files STR.
**Red flags:** Cash deposits, power of attorney, charity used as intermediary, property purchase misaligned with charitable purpose.

### Typology 9: Company Used for ML Through Property
**Pattern:** Company purchase → property → voluntary liquidation → repurchase at higher price.
**Case:** East European national establishes Belgian company, purchases property, voluntarily liquidates the company, then repurchases the same property at a higher price — creating a paper trail of apparent legitimate transactions.
**Red flags:** Rapid buy-liquidate-repurchase cycle, price manipulation, voluntary liquidation with no business rationale.

### Typology 10: Abuse of Notary's Client Account
**Pattern:** Company → property purchase → cheques cashed through notary → sole shareholder = drug trafficker.
**Case:** A company purchases property, with cheques cashed through the notary's client account. Investigation reveals the sole shareholder is a convicted drug trafficker.
**Red flags:** Use of professional's client account, sole shareholder with criminal history, cheque-based payments.

## Responding to Red Flags

### Decision Framework

| Red Flag Assessment | Response |
|-------------------|----------|
| **Single minor flag** in otherwise clean profile | Note it, continue with standard CDD, monitor |
| **Single significant flag** or **2-3 minor flags** | Apply EDD, investigate further, document findings |
| **Multiple significant flags** | Escalate to Compliance Officer immediately, do not proceed |
| **Clear indication of ML/TF** | Escalate immediately, consider filing ISTR, do not proceed |

### What NOT to Do

- Do not ignore flags because the client seems wealthy or legitimate
- Do not dismiss flags because the deal is large or lucrative
- Do not investigate beyond your competence — escalate to the Compliance Officer
- Do not confront the client with your suspicions (tipping off risk)
- Do not delay reporting — document and escalate promptly

## Key Takeaways

1. **Red flags are indicators, not proof** — they signal the need for investigation, not automatic rejection
2. **60+ indicators** across natural persons, legal entities, and transactions — know the categories
3. **Multiple flags compound risk** — one flag warrants attention, several warrant escalation
4. **10 typology case studies** show real ML patterns in real estate — memorise the patterns
5. **Structuring/smurfing, rapid buy/sell, shell companies, family nominees, and price manipulation** are the most common real estate ML methods
6. **Document everything** — your observations, your assessment, and the response you took
