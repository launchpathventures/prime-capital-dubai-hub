# Prime Capital Dubai — Senior Associate Recruitment Strategy

**Draft for Founder Review | February 2026**

---

## Purpose

This document outlines the strategy, messaging, channels, and practical execution steps for recruiting 2–3 Senior Associates into Prime Capital Dubai's new organisational structure.

These are the most important hires Prime Capital will make. The wrong person in this role does more damage than leaving it unfilled. The right person accelerates growth, extends the brand, and brings capability that would take years to build organically.

This is not a standard recruitment exercise. It requires a different approach.

---

## 1. Understanding the Candidate

### Who We're Looking For

These are not people browsing job boards. They are performing well where they are, earning well, and not actively looking to move. They may have thought about going independent but haven't pulled the trigger — either because of the cost, the complexity, or the risk.

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Experience** | 5–8 years in Dubai real estate sales |
| **Track record** | Demonstrable history of high-value transactions, repeat clients, and strong personal revenue |
| **Market cycles** | Ideally active through the 2020–2021 COVID correction — they've seen a downturn and adapted |
| **Reputation** | Known and respected by developers, peers, and clients. A name that carries weight |
| **Ambition** | Wants more than another agency role. Thinks about building something, not just selling |
| **Character** | Professional, discreet, client-first. Not the loudest person in the room but consistently the most effective |
| **Geography** | Currently based in Dubai or recently active in the market |

### What They're Thinking But Not Saying

Understanding their internal monologue is critical to crafting the right message:

- *"I'm doing well, but I'm building someone else's business."*
- *"I've thought about going independent, but the setup cost and hassle puts me off."*
- *"I want to run a team and have a say in how things are done."*
- *"Most agencies are the same — high volume, low quality, no differentiation."*
- *"I want to be associated with a brand I'm actually proud of."*
- *"I don't want to start from scratch — I want infrastructure, not just a desk."*

### What Would Make Them Move

They won't move for a marginal improvement. The proposition needs to be categorically different from what they have now:

1. **Autonomy** — Genuine decision-making authority, not just a title
2. **Economics** — A commission structure that reflects their contribution and experience
3. **Brand** — Association with something distinctive and respected, not another generic agency
4. **Team** — The ability to build and lead their own group
5. **Marketing** — A dedicated budget and input into strategy, not just company-provided leads
6. **Infrastructure** — All the operational support of running an agency, without any of the burden
7. **Recognition** — Public acknowledgment as a leader within the firm

### What Would Stop Them

These objections will surface. Be ready for them:

| Objection | How to address |
|---|---|
| "I'm earning well where I am" | Focus on the ceiling, not the floor. What's the 3-year trajectory here versus there? |
| "I don't know enough about Prime Capital" | This is why the brand and content strategy matters — they need to have seen your work before you approach them |
| "What if it doesn't work out?" | Be transparent about trial periods, notice terms, and what happens if the fit isn't right |
| "I've been burned by promises before" | Don't make promises. Show the structure, the numbers, and the brand. Let the substance speak |
| "I'd rather go independent" | Walk them through the true cost and timeline of setting up — licensing, visa sponsorship, office, compliance, marketing, admin. Then contrast with what Prime Capital provides from day one |

## 2. The Core Message

### Positioning the Opportunity

This is not a job listing. It is an invitation to a different kind of arrangement. The messaging must reflect that.

**The one-sentence pitch:**

> "We've built the infrastructure, the brand, and the client trust. We're looking for someone who wants to build their own operation inside that — with the autonomy of going independent, without the overhead."

**The longer version (for conversations, not ads):**

> "Prime Capital is structured differently from most agencies in Dubai. We're a boutique advisory — small client base, high-value relationships, end-to-end service. We're now looking for 2–3 experienced professionals who want something more than another agency role. The model is this: you run your own book of business, build your own team, shape your own marketing — with a dedicated budget — and represent the Prime Capital brand at developer launches and in the market. You get the autonomy of going independent, with all the infrastructure, compliance, and brand backing handled. If you've thought about setting up on your own but the cost and complexity held you back, this is designed to solve exactly that problem."

### Language Principles for Recruitment Messaging

The same brand voice that applies to client communication applies here. These candidates are sophisticated — they will see through anything that feels like a standard recruitment pitch.

**Do:**

- Be direct about what the role is and what it isn't
- Acknowledge their current success — this isn't a rescue mission
- Frame the conversation as exploratory, not transactional
- Let them ask questions rather than front-loading information
- Be specific about the model — autonomy, team, budget, brand

**Don't:**

- Use generic recruitment language ("exciting opportunity," "dynamic team," "competitive package")
- Oversell or make promises you can't guarantee
- Create false urgency ("we're filling this role fast")
- Position this as doing them a favour — they have options
- Send anything that looks like a mass message

## 3. Recruitment Channels

### Channel 1: Founders' Personal Network

**Why it's the highest-value channel:** A direct, private conversation from Tahir or Zeba carries more weight than any recruiter, DM, or job post. These candidates know the founders by reputation, and a personal approach signals respect and seriousness.

**How to execute:**

1. **Build a shortlist.** Sit down together and list every person in your network who fits the profile — or who is close enough to be worth a conversation. Include people you've worked with, competed against, or seen perform well at developer events. Target 10–15 names.

2. **Prioritise.** Rank by fit, not just capability. The person who is the best salesperson isn't necessarily the best Senior Associate. Prioritise people whose values, professionalism, and client approach align with the Prime Capital brand.

3. **Make contact.** This should be a private, one-to-one conversation — not a group message, not a LinkedIn post. A phone call or in-person coffee. The tone is: *"I wanted to talk to you about something we're building. No pressure, but I think you'd find it interesting."*

4. **Have the conversation.** Don't pitch the role immediately. Start by asking about their situation — what they're working on, what's going well, what frustrates them. Listen for the signals: dissatisfaction with their current agency, interest in building something, frustration with lack of autonomy. Then introduce the concept.

5. **Follow up with substance.** After the conversation, send the structure proposal document and let them digest it. Don't chase. Give them space to think and come back with questions.

**Timeline:** Start immediately. This channel can yield conversations within 1–2 weeks.

**Founder time required:** 2–3 hours per candidate (initial outreach, conversation, follow-up).

---

### Channel 2: Developer Relationship Referrals

**Why it works:** Developers see performance data that agencies don't share publicly. They know who sells well, who delivers a professional client experience, and who has staying power. A trusted developer contact can surface names that wouldn't appear through any other channel.

**How to execute:**

1. **Identify 3–5 developer contacts** with whom you have strong, trust-based relationships. These should be people at a senior enough level to have visibility across their sales channels — Head of Sales, Director of Partnerships, or equivalent.

2. **Have a direct conversation.** Don't send a formal request. Ask informally: *"We're looking to bring on a senior person — someone with real depth, not just volume. Who have you seen consistently perform at a high level? Who do you actually enjoy working with?"*

3. **Ask specifically about character, not just numbers.** Developers can tell you who is professional, who follows through, who represents their projects well to buyers, and who cuts corners. This qualitative input is as valuable as any sales figure.

4. **Handle the referral with discretion.** If a developer gives you a name, do not mention the source when you approach the candidate — unless the developer explicitly offers to make an introduction. Protect the relationship.

5. **Cross-reference with your shortlist.** If a name comes up from multiple developer contacts, that's a strong signal.

**Timeline:** 1–2 weeks to gather names. Then move to direct outreach.

**Risk to manage:** Developers may flag your expansion plans to others in the market. Only approach developers you trust to be discreet.

---

### Channel 3: LinkedIn DM Campaign

**Why it matters:** Reaches candidates outside your immediate network — people you may not know personally but who fit the profile. Also creates a scalable pipeline if the first two channels don't yield enough candidates.

**How to execute:**

#### Step 1: Build a Target List

Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator (or standard LinkedIn search with careful filtering) to identify candidates:

- **Job titles:** Senior Property Consultant, Senior Sales Advisor, Associate Director, Team Leader, Sales Manager — within Dubai real estate agencies
- **Experience:** 5+ years in Dubai real estate
- **Activity signals:** People who post about market analysis, developer projects, or client success (signals engagement and professionalism, not just presence)
- **Company filters:** Focus on agencies with a strong reputation — Betterhomes, Allsopp & Allsopp, Driven Properties, Haus & Haus, Luxhabitat, fäm Properties, and similar. Avoid candidates from agencies with reputations that conflict with the Prime Capital brand.

Target list size: 30–50 profiles. Quality over quantity.

#### Step 2: Research Each Candidate

Before sending any message, review their profile, activity, and public track record. Look for:

- Evidence of transaction volume and quality
- Professional presentation (how they write, what they share)
- Longevity in the market (stayed through tough periods)
- Any red flags (constant agency-hopping, promotional language, hype-based content)

#### Step 3: Send the First Message

The first message must be personal, specific, and brief. It should not look like a mass outreach. No job description, no company pitch, no attachment.

**Template (adapt for each candidate):**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Tahir, one of the founders of Prime Capital Dubai. I came across your profile and your work at [Agency] — your track record in [specific area or segment] stood out.
>
> We're building something a bit different here — a model that gives experienced professionals genuine autonomy, their own team, and a marketing budget, within a boutique advisory structure. It's designed for people who've thought about going independent but want the infrastructure without the overhead.
>
> Not sure if this is something you'd find interesting, but I'd value a 15-minute conversation if you're open to it. No pressure either way.
>
> Tahir

**Key principles:**

- Always send from Tahir or Zeba's personal profile — never a recruiter or company page
- Reference something specific about the candidate — their area focus, a property they listed, their longevity in the market
- Keep it under 100 words
- No attachments, no links, no job descriptions
- The ask is a conversation, not an application

#### Step 4: Follow-Up Sequence

If no response after 5–7 days:

> Hi [Name], just following up on my earlier message. No rush at all — if the timing isn't right, I completely understand. If you're curious about the model, happy to share more over a quick call.

If no response after a further 7 days, move on. Do not send a third message. Persistence beyond two touches crosses into pressure territory, which conflicts with the brand.

#### Step 5: Convert to Conversation

If they respond positively, move to a phone call or in-person meeting as quickly as possible. LinkedIn DMs are for opening the door — the substance happens in conversation.

**Timeline:** 2–3 weeks to build the list, research, and begin outreach. Responses typically come within 1–2 weeks. Allow 4–6 weeks for the full campaign to run.

**Volume:** Expect a 15–25% response rate if messages are genuinely personalised. From 40 outreach messages, that's 6–10 conversations — of which 2–3 may progress to serious interest.

### Channel 4: Industry Events and Developer Launches

**Why it works:** You see people in action. How they carry themselves, how they interact with clients, how they present to developers. This is an observation channel — you're identifying talent, not recruiting on the spot.

**How to execute:**

1. **Attend selectively.** Focus on launches and events where the calibre of agent is likely to be high — premium developers, invitation-only events, industry forums.

2. **Observe and note.** Watch for people who stand out — not the loudest, but the most professional. Who asks intelligent questions? Who seems to have genuine client relationships rather than just chasing leads? Who represents their current agency well?

3. **Make a note, then follow up later.** Don't approach at the event with a pitch. That's unprofessional and puts them in an awkward position in front of peers. Instead, connect on LinkedIn afterward or ask a mutual contact for an introduction.

4. **Use events to build familiarity.** Even if you're not ready to approach someone, being visible at the same events builds recognition. When you do reach out later, you're not a stranger.

**Timeline:** Ongoing. This is a slow channel but surfaces candidates that other methods miss.

---

### Channel 5: Content as a Recruitment Signal

**Why it works:** Prime Capital's YouTube channel and brand presence already communicate a different kind of operation. The right candidates are watching — or will watch when you approach them. Content doesn't replace direct outreach, but it creates credibility that makes every other channel more effective.

**How to execute:**

1. **Consider a specific piece of content** that talks about how Prime Capital operates as a firm — without framing it as recruitment. A video or post about the advisory model, the team structure, or the philosophy behind the brand signals to the market that this is a different kind of organisation.

2. **Share behind-the-scenes content** on LinkedIn. Not polished marketing — authentic glimpses of how the team works. Market analysis sessions, preparation for client meetings, team discussions about strategy. This shows the culture, not just the brand.

3. **Use the YouTube channel as a credibility tool.** When reaching out to candidates, the channel is proof that Prime Capital isn't just another agency. It demonstrates the depth, the positioning, and the quality of thinking. Most candidates will watch several videos before responding.

4. **Do not post a job listing.** A public job ad conflicts with the brand positioning and attracts volume over quality. This is a discreet, invitation-based process.

**Timeline:** Begin immediately. The content pipeline supports recruitment even if the specific hire takes months.

---

### Channel 6: Specialist Recruiter

**Why it exists as an option:** Recruiters have networks and databases that surface candidates you may not find through other channels. They handle the initial screening and reduce the time founders spend on unqualified conversations.

**When to use it:** If channels 1–5 don't yield enough qualified candidates within 6–8 weeks, or if you need to run a broader search while the founders focus on direct outreach.

**How to execute:**

1. **Select a recruiter who specialises in Dubai real estate.** Generalist recruitment firms won't understand the nuance of this role. You need someone who knows the market, the agencies, and the people.

2. **Brief them thoroughly.** Share the structure proposal document, the brand guidelines, and the specific profile you're looking for. Emphasise that this is not a standard sales hire — cultural fit and professional standards matter as much as revenue history.

3. **Set clear expectations on quality versus volume.** You want 5–8 strong, pre-vetted introductions — not a stack of 50 CVs. Every candidate they present should be someone you would be comfortable having represent the Prime Capital brand.

4. **Agree fees upfront.** Standard recruitment fees in Dubai range from one to two months' expected commission. Negotiate a retainer model if possible (payment in stages tied to milestones) rather than pure contingency — this aligns the recruiter's incentive with quality, not speed.

5. **Maintain control of the conversation.** The recruiter makes the introduction, but the founders should lead the substantive discussions. The candidate needs to feel they are being personally invited, not processed through a pipeline.

**Timeline:** 2–4 weeks for recruiter briefing and initial candidate identification. 4–8 weeks for introductions and conversations.

**Cost:** Expect AED 30,000–60,000 per placement depending on the commission level of the role.

---

### Channel 7: Discreet Expression of Interest Page

**Why it works:** Creates a low-friction way for interested professionals to signal curiosity — without the formality of a job application. This catches people who hear about the opportunity through word of mouth, see the content, or are referred informally.

**How to execute:**

1. **Create a single page** on the Prime Capital website — not linked from the main navigation. Accessible only via a direct URL that you share selectively.

2. **Position it as an invitation, not a job listing.** The copy should describe the model, the philosophy, and what Prime Capital is looking for — without reading like a recruitment ad.

3. **Keep the form minimal.** Name, email, phone, and a single open-ended question: *"Tell us briefly about your experience and what interests you about this model."* No CV upload required at this stage.

4. **Share the link selectively** — in LinkedIn DMs, in follow-up emails after conversations, and with anyone who refers a potential candidate. Do not post it publicly.

**Suggested page copy:**

> **A Different Kind of Proposition**
>
> Prime Capital Dubai is a boutique real estate advisory serving high-net-worth international investors. We are expanding our senior team and looking for experienced professionals who want genuine autonomy — their own book of business, their own team, a dedicated marketing budget — within a firm that provides full infrastructure, operational support, and a brand built on substance, not noise.
>
> This is designed for people with 5–8 years of Dubai experience who have considered going independent but want the platform without the overhead.
>
> If this sounds like something worth exploring, we'd welcome a conversation.

**Timeline:** Can be built and live within a week. Serves as a passive capture channel alongside active outreach.

## 4. The Conversation Framework

Every channel ultimately leads to a conversation. How that conversation is structured determines whether a strong candidate moves forward or walks away.

### The First Conversation (30–45 minutes)

**Objective:** Understand their situation, share the model at a high level, and assess mutual interest. This is not an interview — it is a two-way exploration.

**Structure:**

**Open with curiosity (10 minutes):**
- What are you working on right now?
- How long have you been at [current agency]?
- What's going well? What would you change if you could?
- Have you thought about going independent?

*Listen more than you talk. The goal is to understand their motivations, frustrations, and ambitions.*

**Introduce the model (10 minutes):**
- Share the Prime Capital positioning — boutique advisory, high-net-worth clients, end-to-end service
- Explain the Senior Associate structure — autonomy, team, marketing budget, brand representation
- Be specific about what's on offer and what the expectations are
- Use the phrase: *"We've built the infrastructure and the brand. We're looking for someone who wants to build their own operation inside that."*

**Address their questions (10 minutes):**
- Let them lead. Whatever they ask reveals what matters most to them
- Be transparent about economics, expectations, and how the arrangement works in practice
- If they ask about commission, be direct. Vagueness at this stage creates suspicion

**Close with next steps (5 minutes):**
- If there's mutual interest: *"I'd like to share our structure document with you — it lays out the model in detail. Take your time with it, and let's reconnect in a week or so."*
- If the fit isn't right: *"I appreciate the conversation. If your situation changes, or if you know someone who might find this interesting, I'd welcome the introduction."*

### The Second Conversation (45–60 minutes)

**Objective:** Go deeper on the model, discuss economics, and assess fit on both sides.

**Topics to cover:**

- Their reaction to the structure document — what excited them, what concerned them
- Commission structure — be specific about percentages, team overrides, and how the economics work
- Marketing budget — how much, how it's allocated, what decisions they can make independently
- Team building — timeline for hiring, recruitment support, how the team structure works
- Performance expectations — what targets look like, how they're measured, what happens if targets aren't met
- Brand standards — walk them through the voice guidelines and the positioning. Gauge their reaction. Do they naturally align, or does it feel like a constraint?
- Contractual terms — notice period, non-solicitation, client ownership. Be upfront

### The Third Conversation (Decision Stage)

**Objective:** Final alignment and offer discussion.

**By this point, both sides should know whether the fit is right.** If either party is uncertain, it's better to pause than to force a decision.

- Confirm the terms — commission, budget, title, start date
- Discuss the first 90 days — what does a successful start look like?
- Address any remaining concerns directly
- If they're ready, make the offer. If they need time, give it — but set a clear timeline for a decision

## 5. Evaluation Criteria

### How to Assess Candidates

Not every strong salesperson is a strong Senior Associate. The role requires a broader set of capabilities. Evaluate against these five dimensions:

| Dimension | What to look for | Red flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Market depth** | Can they talk about market cycles, area dynamics, and developer quality with specificity and nuance? Do they have genuine insight, or are they repeating headlines? | Vague or surface-level market knowledge. Over-reliance on hype or headline numbers. |
| **Client orientation** | How do they describe their client relationships? Do they talk about understanding client needs, or about closing deals? | Transactional language. Focus on volume over quality. Pressure-based selling. |
| **Professional standards** | How do they present themselves — in conversation, in writing, in public? Would you be comfortable with them representing Prime Capital to a sophisticated investor? | Flashy, loud, or unprofessional presentation. Language that conflicts with the brand (hype, pressure, superlatives). |
| **Leadership potential** | Have they mentored or managed others? How do they talk about team dynamics? Do they elevate others or compete with them? | No interest in team building. Individual-focused mindset. Difficulty sharing credit. |
| **Cultural alignment** | Do they naturally connect with the Prime Capital philosophy — substance over shine, advisory over sales, transparency over persuasion? Or does it feel forced? | Lip service to values without evidence. History of agency-hopping. Reputation for aggressive tactics. |

### The Brand Alignment Test

Before making an offer, ask yourself:

- Would I trust this person to sit across from a $5M investor and represent Prime Capital without supervision?
- Would their LinkedIn content, communication style, and professional presence strengthen or dilute the brand?
- If a sceptical prospect looked them up, would what they found reinforce Prime Capital's positioning?

If the answer to any of these is "no" or "I'm not sure," the candidate is not right — regardless of their sales numbers.

## 6. Execution Timeline

### Phase 1: Preparation (Weeks 1–2)

- Finalise the organisational structure document for sharing with candidates
- Build the founder shortlist (Channel 1) — 10–15 names
- Identify 3–5 developer contacts for referral conversations (Channel 2)
- Build the LinkedIn target list (Channel 3) — 30–50 profiles
- Create the expression of interest page (Channel 7)
- Draft and refine all outreach messaging

### Phase 2: Active Outreach (Weeks 3–6)

- Begin founder-led personal conversations (Channel 1) — 3–5 per week
- Have developer referral conversations (Channel 2)
- Launch LinkedIn DM campaign (Channel 3) — 8–10 personalised messages per week
- Attend any relevant industry events (Channel 4)
- Publish content that signals the opportunity indirectly (Channel 5)

### Phase 3: Conversations and Assessment (Weeks 5–10)

- Conduct first conversations with interested candidates
- Share structure document with serious prospects
- Conduct second conversations with shortlisted candidates
- Assess against evaluation criteria

### Phase 4: Decision and Offer (Weeks 8–12)

- Final conversations with top 2–3 candidates
- Make offers
- Allow reasonable decision time (1–2 weeks)
- Begin onboarding planning for accepted candidates

### Phase 5: Recruiter Activation (If Needed — Week 6+)

- If channels 1–5 haven't surfaced enough qualified candidates by week 6, brief a specialist recruiter
- Run recruiter search in parallel with ongoing direct efforts

## 7. What Success Looks Like

### By Week 12

- 15–25 meaningful conversations held
- 5–8 candidates assessed against the full evaluation criteria
- 2–3 offers made to candidates who meet the standard across all five dimensions
- At least 1 accepted offer with a confirmed start date

### By Month 6

- First Senior Associate fully operational — managing own clients, building team, active in developer relationships
- Revenue contribution beginning to materialise
- Brand alignment validated through client feedback and market perception
- Model proven enough to inform the second and third hire

### By Month 12

- 2–3 Senior Associates in place
- Each managing a small team (1–2 Associates or Junior Associates)
- Measurable revenue contribution from each team
- Prime Capital's market presence noticeably expanded without any dilution of the brand

## 8. Key Risks in the Recruitment Process

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Hiring too fast** | Wrong person damages the brand and costs more than an unfilled role | Maintain standards. It's better to take 6 months to find the right person than to compromise in month 2. |
| **Hiring from within the network only** | Misses strong candidates outside the founders' circle | Use LinkedIn and developer referrals alongside personal network. |
| **Overselling the opportunity** | Candidate joins with inflated expectations, becomes disillusioned quickly | Be transparent about what's in place and what's still being built. Honesty in recruitment prevents problems in retention. |
| **Losing momentum** | Outreach starts strong then stalls as founders get busy with other priorities | Block dedicated time for recruitment activities. Treat this as a strategic priority, not a side project. |
| **Market visibility** | Competitors learn about the expansion plans and counter-offer to retain their people | Keep the process discreet. Don't announce publicly. Use private channels and personal conversations. |

---

## 9. Summary

The Senior Associate hire is not a standard recruitment exercise. The candidates Prime Capital needs are not looking for jobs — they need to be found, approached personally, and shown something they haven't seen before.

The strategy works across seven channels, but the priority is clear: founder-led personal conversations and developer referrals will surface the strongest candidates. LinkedIn DM extends the reach. Everything else is supporting infrastructure.

The messaging must be consistent across all channels: this is an invitation to build something, not a job offer. The tone is confident but not pushy, specific but not salesy — the same standard Prime Capital applies to everything client-facing.

The single most important principle: do not compromise on quality. Two exceptional Senior Associates will outperform five mediocre ones. Protect the standard, and the model will work.
