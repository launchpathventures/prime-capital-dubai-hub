# AML Compliance Quiz: KYC for Corporate & Legal Entities

**Purpose:** Verify your ability to complete corporate KYC, identify Ultimate Beneficial Owners, and handle the additional requirements for developer and broker onboarding.

**Format:** 5 multiple-choice questions
**Passing Score:** 80% (4 out of 5 correct)
**Time Limit:** No time limit (typically 8 minutes)
**Retakes:** Unlimited

---

## Questions

### Question 1
**Company Alpha is owned 40% by Company Beta (Cayman Islands) and 60% by Mr. Khalid (UAE resident). Company Beta is owned 100% by Ms. Chen (Singapore resident). Who are the UBOs of Company Alpha?**

A) Only Mr. Khalid — he owns the majority
B) Mr. Khalid (60% direct ownership) and Ms. Chen (40% indirect ownership) — both exceed 25%
C) Company Beta — it owns 40%
D) Mr. Khalid only — Ms. Chen's ownership is too indirect

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** UBO identification traces to natural persons. Mr. Khalid directly owns 60% — clearly a UBO. Ms. Chen indirectly owns 40% of Company Alpha (100% of Company Beta × 40% = 40%) — also exceeds the 25% threshold and is a UBO. Companies cannot be UBOs — only natural persons qualify. Both Mr. Khalid and Ms. Chen require full KYC documentation.

### Question 2
**A corporate client is itself a law firm (a DNFBP). What additional document must you collect beyond the standard 12 corporate documents?**

A) A professional indemnity insurance certificate
B) An AML/CFT policy and procedures document or AML questionnaire
C) A reference letter from their bank
D) No additional documents — the standard 12 are sufficient for all corporate clients

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** When the corporate client is a DNFBP (law firm, accounting practice, real estate firm, etc.), Prime Capital requires an AML/CFT questionnaire or their AML policy and procedures. This questionnaire (15+ questions) assesses whether the DNFBP has its own AML controls — because their AML deficiencies become Prime Capital's risk exposure. This is document #11 in the 12-document list.

### Question 3
**How many documents are required for corporate client onboarding at Prime Capital?**

A) 7 — same as individual clients
B) 10 — KYC form, IDs, trade license, and financial documents
C) 12 — including KYC form, trade license, MOA, VAT certificate, UBO IDs, address proof, financial documents, AML questionnaire, and UBO register
D) 15 — corporate clients require additional regulatory filings

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Corporate onboarding requires 12 documents: (1) KYC form, (2) Source of fund undertaking, (3) Trade license/certificate of incorporation, (4) MOA/POA, (5) VAT registration, (6) UBO/owner IDs + POA holder ID, (7) Address proof, (8) Source of funds document, (9) Source of wealth documentation, (10) Third-party undertaking, (11) AML policy/questionnaire (if DNFBP), and (12) UBO register.

### Question 4
**When onboarding an external real estate broker for a co-brokerage deal, which additional documents are required beyond standard corporate KYC?**

A) Developer registration certificate and escrow account details
B) RERA license, broker RERA card, and agent-to-agent agreement
C) Personal bank statements of the broker and proof of insurance
D) Only the broker's business card and verbal confirmation of license

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** External broker onboarding requires three additional documents: (1) valid company RERA license, (2) individual broker RERA card, and (3) a formal agent-to-agent agreement. These ensure the broker is properly licensed and the co-brokerage arrangement is documented, plus all standard corporate KYC requirements apply.

### Question 5
**You are onboarding a company and discover the shares are held by two professional nominee companies. Neither nominee is the real owner. What should you do?**

A) Accept the nominee companies as the UBOs since they legally hold the shares
B) Trace ownership behind the nominees to identify the natural persons who ultimately benefit, using nominee agreements
C) Decline the relationship — nominee structures indicate money laundering
D) Request a letter from the nominee companies confirming they are the beneficial owners

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Nominees are not the UBO — they hold shares on behalf of someone else. You must look behind the nominee structure by requesting nominee agreements and tracing ownership to the natural person who ultimately benefits. Nominee structures are not inherently suspicious (they are common in some jurisdictions for privacy or tax reasons), but they require thorough investigation. If you cannot identify the natural person behind the nominees, escalate to the Compliance Officer.
