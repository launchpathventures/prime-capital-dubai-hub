# KYC for Corporate & Legal Entities

## Introduction

Corporate clients introduce layers of complexity that individual clients do not. Behind every company is a chain of ownership that must be traced to identify the natural person who ultimately owns or controls it — the Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO). This module covers the corporate KYC form, the 12 required documents, UBO identification procedures, and the additional requirements for developer and external broker onboarding.

**Critical point:** Corporate structures are one of the most common vehicles for money laundering. Shell companies, layered holding entities, and complex ownership chains exist specifically to obscure the true owner. Your job is to see through the structure to the person behind it.

## Why This Matters

A significant proportion of high-value property transactions in Dubai involve corporate buyers — free zone companies, holding companies, family offices, and international entities. Each of these requires more thorough KYC than an individual purchase. Getting corporate KYC wrong exposes the firm to the highest-risk ML scenarios.

## Corporate KYC Form — Required Fields

### Entity Information
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Full legal name** | Exactly as registered |
| **Legal form** | LLC, free zone company, public joint company, etc. |
| **Licensing authority** | DED, free zone authority, foreign registrar |
| **Trade license number** | With issue and expiry dates |
| **Business address** | Registered office address |
| **Contact details** | Phone, email, website |
| **Date/place/country of incorporation** | Where and when the entity was formed |

### Business Activity
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Core business activities** | Primary line of business |
| **Main products/services** | What the company actually does |
| **Number of subsidiaries** | Plus group chart if applicable |
| **Purpose of transaction** | Why the company is purchasing/selling property |

### Financial Information
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Source of funds** | Mandatory for cash/wire ≥ AED 55,000 and non-cash ≥ AED 300,000 |
| **Source of wealth** | How the company accumulated its assets |
| **Turnover details** | Revenue and financial scale |
| **External auditors** | Who audits the company's accounts |
| **Tax identification/registration number** | TRN or equivalent |
| **Bank details** | Primary banking relationship |

### Ownership & Control
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Shareholder/owner details** | Name and percentage holding for each shareholder |
| **UBO details** | Name, address, nationality, DOB, percentage holding for each UBO |
| **Management structure** | Board members, senior management, authorised signatories |

### Compliance
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **AML/CFT questionnaire** | Required if the entity is a DNFBP |
| **PEP declaration** | For shareholders, board members, and senior management |
| **Source of fund undertaking** | Signed |
| **General declaration** | Signed |

## Documents for Corporate Clients (12 Items)

| # | Document | Notes |
|---|----------|-------|
| 1 | **KYC form** | System-based or manual, fully completed |
| 2 | **Source of fund undertaking** | Signed by authorised signatory |
| 3 | **Valid trade license / certificate of incorporation** | Current, not expired |
| 4 | **Memorandum & Articles of Association / POA** | Constitutional documents of the entity |
| 5 | **VAT registration certificate** | If VAT-registered |
| 6 | **Valid IDs of owners/partners (UBOs) + POA holder** | Passport/EID for all UBOs and any POA holders |
| 7 | **Tenancy contract / address proof / utility bill** | Proof of registered address |
| 8 | **Source of funds document** | Balance sheet, bank statement, or VAT return — required for cash transactions, PEPs, and high-risk (EDD) |
| 9 | **Source of wealth documentation** | Required for PEPs and high-risk clients (EDD) |
| 10 | **Third-party undertaking + supporting docs** | If a third party is involved in the transaction |
| 11 | **AML policy & procedures / AML questionnaire** | Required if the entity is a DNFBP |
| 12 | **UBO register** | Official register of beneficial owners |

## UBO Identification

### The 25% Rule

Under Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023, the Ultimate Beneficial Owner is:

**Primary test:** Any natural person who owns 25% or more of the shares OR controls 25% or more of the voting rights — directly or indirectly.

### The Cascade

When no single person meets the 25% threshold, apply these steps in order:

1. **Aggregate ownership** — If multiple persons share ownership that aggregates to 25% or more, all are treated as owners/controllers
2. **Control through other means** — If no natural person is identified through ownership, look for persons who exercise control through management authority, contractual arrangements, or other mechanisms
3. **Highest-ranking management** — If still no natural person can be identified, the highest-ranking management official is deemed the UBO

### Escalation Rule

If each legal person or arrangement in the ownership chain holds less than 25%, but common ownership patterns could aggregate to 25% or more, **escalate to the Compliance Officer** for assessment.

### Exemptions

Two categories of entities are exempt from UBO identification requirements:

1. **Government-owned entities** — Entities directly or indirectly wholly owned by the federal or emirate government
2. **Listed companies** — Entities listed on regulated stock exchanges in Europe, US, UK, or GCC — but you must document evidence of the exchange's disclosure requirements

### Practical Example

**Company A** (the buyer) is a Dubai free zone company.
- Company A is owned 60% by **Company B** (BVI) and 40% by **Company C** (UK).
- Company B is owned 100% by **Mr. X** (Lebanese national, Dubai resident).
- Company C is owned 50% by **Mr. Y** (British national) and 50% by **Mrs. Z** (British national).

**UBO analysis:**
- Mr. X owns 60% of Company A indirectly → UBO (exceeds 25%)
- Mr. Y owns 20% of Company A indirectly (50% of 40%) → Not a UBO individually
- Mrs. Z owns 20% of Company A indirectly → Not a UBO individually
- However, Mr. Y and Mrs. Z together own 40% → May need to be treated as co-controllers

**Action:** Mr. X is clearly a UBO. For Mr. Y and Mrs. Z, assess whether they act together as controllers. Collect identification for all three and escalate the aggregation question to the Compliance Officer.

## Developer Onboarding (Policy s.9.4)

When Prime Capital engages with a developer (to list their projects, represent their off-plan sales, etc.), additional KYC is required beyond the standard corporate form:

| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Commercial license** | Valid developer license |
| **Memorandum of Association** | Constitutional documents |
| **UBO identification documents** | For all beneficial owners |
| **POA for authorised signatory** | Authorisation to act on behalf of developer |
| **Tax registration certificate** | TRN |
| **Signed agency agreement** | Between Prime Capital and the developer |

All standard corporate KYC fields and documents also apply. The developer onboarding combines the corporate KYC requirements with these additional developer-specific documents.

---

## External Agency/Broker Onboarding (Policy s.9.5)

When Prime Capital collaborates with an external agency or broker (agent-to-agent deals), additional verification is required:

| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **RERA license** | Valid company RERA registration |
| **Broker RERA card** | Individual broker registration |
| **Agent-to-agent agreement** | Formal co-brokerage agreement |

Plus all standard corporate KYC requirements. This ensures the external broker is properly licensed and their commission structures are documented.

## AML/CFT Questionnaire for DNFBP Clients

When the corporate client is itself a DNFBP (another real estate firm, a law firm, an accounting practice, etc.), Prime Capital requires them to complete an **AML/CFT questionnaire**. This questionnaire covers 15+ yes/no questions about their own AML programme:

- Do they have a written AML/CFT policy?
- Have they appointed a Compliance Officer/MLRO?
- Do they conduct CDD on their clients?
- Do they screen against sanctions lists?
- Do they provide AML training to staff?
- Have they been subject to any AML-related regulatory action?

**Why this matters:** If a DNFBP client does not have adequate AML controls, transacting with them increases Prime Capital's risk exposure. Their AML deficiencies become our AML risk.

## Common Corporate KYC Challenges

### Complex Multi-Layered Structures
**Challenge:** Client presents a holding company owned by another holding company owned by a trust.
**Response:** Trace the ownership chain to the natural person. Request group charts, constitutional documents for each entity, and identification for all UBOs. If the structure is deliberately opaque, escalate to the Compliance Officer.

### Nominee Shareholders
**Challenge:** Shares are held by professional nominee companies.
**Response:** Nominees are not the UBO — you must identify the person behind the nominee. Request nominee agreements and trace to the natural person who ultimately benefits.

### Bearer Shares
**Challenge:** The entity has issued bearer shares (ownership is determined by physical possession of share certificates).
**Response:** Bearer shares are a high-risk indicator. Identify who holds the certificates and verify their identity. Escalate to the Compliance Officer — this is an EDD trigger.

### Foreign Entities with No UAE Presence
**Challenge:** A foreign company with no physical office or business in the UAE wants to purchase property.
**Response:** Collect all standard documentation plus verify the entity's registration in its home jurisdiction. This is inherently higher risk — assess whether the purpose of the transaction is consistent with the entity's declared activities.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Corporate KYC requires 12 documents** — more than double the individual requirement
2. **UBO identification follows the 25% rule** with a cascade when no one meets the threshold
3. **Trace ownership to the natural person** — companies do not launder money, people do
4. **Developer onboarding** adds commercial license, MOA, UBO IDs, POA, tax certificate, and agency agreement
5. **External broker onboarding** adds RERA license, broker card, and agent-to-agent agreement
6. **DNFBP clients** must complete the AML/CFT questionnaire about their own compliance programme
7. **Complex structures are not inherently suspicious** — but they require thorough documentation and potentially escalation
