# Risk Assessment & Customer Risk Rating

## Introduction

Not all clients carry the same level of risk. A UAE-resident salaried employee purchasing a home is fundamentally different from a non-resident self-employed individual from a FATF grey-list country purchasing through a corporate vehicle. The **risk-based approach** ensures you apply proportionate measures — more scrutiny where risk is higher, streamlined processes where risk is lower.

This module covers how risk assessment works at Prime Capital: the three levels of assessment, the specific risk rating tables you will use to classify clients, the prohibited client categories, and the risk factors specific to real estate transactions.

## Why This Matters

Risk assessment is the gateway to everything else in AML compliance. The risk rating you assign to a client determines:

- Whether they receive Simplified Due Diligence (SDD), standard CDD, or Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
- How frequently their CDD must be reviewed (every 6 months, annually, or every 2 years)
- The level of ongoing monitoring applied to their transactions
- Whether senior management approval is needed for the relationship

**Get the risk rating wrong, and every subsequent step is wrong.** Under-rating a high-risk client means insufficient due diligence. Over-rating a low-risk client wastes resources and damages the client experience.

---

## The Risk-Based Approach

### Why One-Size-Fits-All Doesn't Work

Applying identical AML procedures to every client is both inefficient and ineffective:

- **Too much scrutiny on low-risk clients** damages relationships and wastes time
- **Too little scrutiny on high-risk clients** exposes the firm to regulatory and criminal liability
- **Resources are finite** — they must be concentrated where the risk is highest

The risk-based approach, required by FATF Recommendation 1 and UAE law, means you assess each client's risk profile and apply measures proportionate to that risk.

## Three Levels of Risk Assessment

### Level 1: Enterprise-Wide Risk Assessment (EWRA)

**What it is:** An annual assessment of ML/TF/PF risks across the entire firm.

**Who does it:** The Compliance Officer, with input from senior management.

**Parameters assessed (5):**

1. **Customer risk** — Types of clients the firm serves (individuals, corporates, PEPs, non-residents)
2. **Geographic risk** — Countries and jurisdictions the firm deals with
3. **Delivery channel risk** — How services are delivered (face-to-face, remote, through intermediaries)
4. **Product/service risk** — Types of transactions facilitated (off-plan, secondary, commercial, residential)
5. **Transaction risk** — Volume, value, and patterns of transactions

**Your role:** You contribute data to the EWRA through your client interactions — the patterns you observe, the client types you encounter, and the geographies you deal with inform the firm-level assessment.

### Level 2: Product/Service/New Technology Risk Assessment

**What it is:** Assessment of risks specific to particular products, services, or new technologies the firm uses or encounters.

**Examples:**
- Off-plan property sales (higher risk due to longer timelines and payment structures)
- Cryptocurrency or virtual asset payments (higher risk due to anonymity)
- Use of power of attorney (higher risk due to non-face-to-face element)

### Level 3: Customer Relationship (Business) Level Risk Assessment

**What it is:** The risk assessment you perform for each individual client. This is the level you interact with daily.

**Customer risk factors:**

| Factor | What to Assess |
|--------|---------------|
| **Customer type** | Individual, corporate, trust, NPO, government entity |
| **Profession/Business** | Employment type, industry sector, cash intensity |
| **Country** | Nationality, country of domicile, country of operations |
| **Source of funds/income** | Origin of the money being used in the transaction |
| **Transaction size/volume** | Value and frequency of transactions |
| **Screening/adverse media** | Results of sanctions screening and media checks |

## Prime Capital's Customer Risk Rating Tables

**These are the specific risk rating tables from Prime Capital's AML/CFT Policy (Section 10). You must use these exact classifications.**

### Individual Clients

| Risk Level | Client Profile |
|------------|---------------|
| **Low** | Retired, pensioner, unemployed, or salaried person residing in the UAE — **except** nationals of Syria, Sudan, Cuba, Iran, or Russia |
| **Medium** | Housewife, dependent, or self-employed person residing in the UAE; Salaried UAE national of Sudan, Syria, or Cuba — **except** Iran and Russia nationals |
| **High** | Iran and Russian nationals residing in the UAE; Self-employed person with a high-risk business line; Politically Exposed Person (PEP); Non-resident (regardless of nationality) |

### Entity Clients

| Risk Level | Entity Type |
|------------|-------------|
| **Low** | Government entity, public joint company |
| **Medium** | Food manufacturing, car parts, furniture, cafe/restaurant, electronics, supermarket, clinic/pharmacy, salon |
| **High** | Tour & travel, PEP-owned company, consultancy, cash-intensive business, exchange house, insurance, oil & gas trading, car dealer, import/export, precious metals/luxury goods, assets management, lawyer firm, holding company, shipping, registered NPO/trust, auction house, general trading, free zone company |

### How to Use These Tables

**Step 1:** Determine the customer type (individual or entity)

**Step 2:** Match the client to the appropriate category using the table

**Step 3:** Assign the corresponding risk level (Low, Medium, or High)

**Step 4:** Apply the appropriate due diligence level:
- Low → Simplified Due Diligence (SDD) may apply
- Medium → Standard Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
- High → Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) in addition to standard CDD

**Important:** These tables provide the starting point. Additional factors (screening results, transaction patterns, source of funds concerns) can increase the risk rating but should not decrease it.

## Prohibited Client Types

The following client types must **never** be onboarded at Prime Capital. There is no exception, override, or senior management approval that can change this:

| Prohibited Type | Reason |
|----------------|--------|
| **North Korea (DPRK) nationals** | FATF blacklist, comprehensive sanctions |
| **Myanmar nationals** | FATF blacklist |
| **Arms dealers** | Inherently high ML/TF risk |
| **Unlicensed hawala operators** | Unregulated money transfer — primary ML channel |
| **Persons involved in drug dealing** | Predicate offence for ML |
| **Unlicensed gambling or casino operators** | Cash-intensive, unregulated |
| **Unregistered NPOs or trusts** | Potential TF channel without regulatory oversight |
| **Persons with direct SDN links** | Specially Designated Nationals — sanctions violations |

**If you identify a potential client as falling into any of these categories, you must:**
1. Decline the relationship immediately
2. Notify the Compliance Officer
3. Document the interaction

## ML Risk Factors Specific to Real Estate

Prime Capital's Policy (Section 8.5) identifies **8 ML risk factors** particularly relevant to real estate transactions:

1. **High-value cash transactions** — Large amounts of physical cash involved in property purchases
2. **Complex ownership structures** — Multiple layers of companies, trusts, or nominees obscuring the true buyer
3. **Third-party payments** — Someone other than the named buyer provides funds with no clear relationship
4. **Rapid purchase and resale** — Properties bought and sold quickly, especially at unusual prices
5. **Non-face-to-face transactions** — Buyer never appears in person, operates entirely through representatives
6. **High-risk jurisdictions** — Funds originating from or transactions involving FATF grey/blacklist countries
7. **Over- or under-valuation** — Transaction price significantly different from market value
8. **Unusual source of funds** — Source of funds does not match the client's known profile or declared occupation

---

## TF Risk Factors

Five risk factors indicate potential terrorist financing in real estate:

1. **Frequent low-value transactions** — Multiple small transactions that individually fall below reporting thresholds
2. **Charitable organisation links** — Funds flowing through NPOs or charities with connections to conflict zones
3. **Conflict zone funds** — Money originating from or destined for areas of armed conflict
4. **Lack of economic justification** — Transactions that make no commercial sense (buying at above market value, purchasing properties with no intention to use or rent)
5. **Anonymous donors** — Funds from unidentified sources or individuals who cannot be verified

---

## PF Risk Factors

Four risk factors indicate potential proliferation financing:

1. **Sanctioned jurisdiction entities** — Companies or individuals based in countries subject to comprehensive sanctions (North Korea, Iran)
2. **Shell or front companies** — Entities with no genuine business activity used to move funds
3. **Dual-use goods involvement** — Clients whose businesses involve materials or technologies that could be used for WMD development
4. **Obscured end beneficiaries** — Transaction structures designed to hide the ultimate beneficiary

## Cygnus Scan — Automated Risk Assessment

Prime Capital uses **Cygnus Scan** as its screening and risk assessment tool:

- Generates automated risk assessments from client KYC details
- Screens against sanctions lists, PEP databases, and adverse media
- Provides risk scores that feed into the customer risk rating
- Integrates with the onboarding process

**Your role:** You input client information during onboarding; Cygnus Scan generates the screening results and risk assessment. You must review these results and take appropriate action based on the output.

## National and Sectoral Risk Assessments

Two additional assessments inform Prime Capital's approach:

### National Risk Assessment (NRA)
The UAE's government-level assessment of ML/TF/PF risks across the country. This feeds into Prime Capital's EWRA and helps identify national-level threats and vulnerabilities.

### Sectoral Risk Assessment (SRA)
The Ministry of Economy's assessment of ML/TF/PF risks specific to the real estate sector. Identifies the particular risks, typologies, and vulnerabilities relevant to real estate transactions.

Both assessments are incorporated into Prime Capital's enterprise-wide risk assessment and influence the firm's policies and procedures.

## CDD Review Frequencies

Based on the assigned risk level, customer due diligence must be reviewed at these intervals:

| Risk Level | Review Frequency |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Low** | Every 2 years |
| **Medium** | Every 1 year (annually) |
| **High** | Every 6 months |

**Trigger-based reviews:** In addition to scheduled reviews, CDD must be updated whenever:
- There is a significant change in the client's circumstances
- A suspicious indicator is identified
- The client's risk profile changes (e.g., they become a PEP)
- New adverse media or screening results emerge

## Key Takeaways

1. **The risk-based approach** applies proportionate measures — more scrutiny where risk is higher
2. **Three levels of risk assessment** exist: enterprise-wide, product/service, and customer-level
3. **Prime Capital's risk rating tables** are specific — memorise the individual and entity classifications
4. **Prohibited clients** are absolute — North Korea/Myanmar nationals, arms dealers, unlicensed hawala, drug dealers, gambling operators, unregistered NPOs, and SDN-linked persons
5. **8 ML risk factors** are specific to real estate — high-value cash, complex structures, third-party payments, rapid buy/sell, non-face-to-face, high-risk jurisdictions, valuation anomalies, unusual source of funds
6. **CDD review frequencies** are mandatory: Low = 2 years, Medium = 1 year, High = 6 months
7. **Cygnus Scan** automates screening and risk assessment — but you must review and act on the results
