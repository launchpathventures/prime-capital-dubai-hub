# PEPs & UBO Identification

## Introduction

Two categories of clients require special attention in AML compliance: Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and transactions involving complex beneficial ownership. PEPs carry inherent corruption and ML risk due to their access to public funds and influence. Complex ownership structures can obscure who truly benefits from a transaction. This module covers identification procedures for both.

## Why This Matters

PEPs are not inherently criminal — many are legitimate investors. But their positions make them vulnerable to bribery, corruption, and misuse of public funds. History is full of examples of political figures laundering state funds through real estate. Your obligation is not to refuse all PEPs but to apply appropriate enhanced measures.

Similarly, UBO identification is not about suspecting corporate clients — it is about ensuring you know who you are actually dealing with. A company cannot launder money. Only the person behind it can.

---

## Politically Exposed Persons — Who Qualifies?

### Definition

A PEP is any individual entrusted with a **prominent public function**, whether in the UAE or a foreign country. Specific roles include:

- **Heads of state or government** (presidents, prime ministers, rulers)
- **Senior politicians** (ministers, deputy ministers, parliamentary members)
- **Senior government officials** (directors general, heads of government agencies)
- **Judicial officials** (supreme court judges, senior members of the judiciary)
- **Military officials** (senior military commanders, defence officials)
- **Senior executives of state-owned corporations** (CEOs and board members of government companies)
- **Senior officials of political parties** (party leaders, treasurers)
- **Persons managing international organisations** (UN, IMF, World Bank senior officials)

### PEP Categories

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Government roles** | Legislative bodies, executive bodies, diplomatic roles, judiciary, state-owned enterprises | Minister of Finance, Ambassador, Supreme Court Justice |
| **Organisations** | Central financial institutions, armed forces (high-ranking), international sports committees | Central Bank Governor, Army General, FIFA Executive Committee |

### The Three PEP Categories for Prime Capital Screening

1. **Foreign PEPs** — Persons holding prominent positions in foreign countries
2. **Domestic PEPs** — UAE government and public sector officials
3. **International Organisation PEPs** — Senior executives of international organisations (UN, World Bank, etc.)
4. **Related Persons** — Close associates and family members of any of the above

---

## PEP Family Members and Close Associates

PEP status extends beyond the individual. The following persons are also treated as PEPs:

### Direct Family
- **Spouses** (current)
- **Children** (including adult children)
- **Spouses of children**
- **Parents**

### Extended Family
- **Siblings**
- **Uncles and aunts**
- **In-laws**

### Close Associates
- Persons with **joint beneficial ownership** of a legal entity or arrangement with the PEP
- Persons who are the **sole beneficial owner** of a legal entity or arrangement set up for the PEP's benefit

**Example:** A government minister's adult son purchases a Dubai apartment. The son is a PEP by family association — even if he has no political role himself. All PEP procedures apply, including EDD and senior management approval.

## Prime Capital PEP Procedures (Policy s.9.8.2)

### a) Risk-Based Assessment

PEP risk assessment is performed:
- At **onboarding** — as part of initial client intake
- **Throughout the relationship** — PEP status can change (client becomes a PEP, or PEP is identified through new information)

### b) Screening — Cygnus Scan

Cygnus Scan screens all clients against global PEP databases:
- At **onboarding** — initial screening
- **Periodically** — regular re-screening at review intervals
- On **new information or alerts** — when adverse media or regulatory updates emerge

### c) Identification and Verification

When PEP status is confirmed (self-declaration or screening):
- Integrated into CDD process with **additional EDD measures**
- **Verify the political function** — what position does/did the person hold?
- **Establish source of funds** — with documentary evidence, not just declarations
- **Establish source of wealth** — how was their wealth accumulated?
- **Understand business purpose** — why are they purchasing property in Dubai?
- **Obtain senior management approval** — mandatory, cannot be delegated

### d) Continuous Monitoring

PEP relationships receive enhanced ongoing monitoring:
- **Re-screening at regular intervals** — more frequent than standard clients
- **Transaction monitoring** — watching for unusual patterns, unexpected transaction volumes, or activity inconsistent with declared purpose
- **Adverse media monitoring** — checking for news reports linking the PEP to corruption, investigations, or sanctions

### e) Risk Mitigation and Escalation

- **All PEP relationships** are escalated to the Compliance Officer for review
- Relationships may be **declined or terminated** if transparency or legitimacy cannot be established
- The Compliance Officer makes the final determination on whether to proceed

---

## Senior Management Approval — Mandatory Scenarios

Senior management approval is **required** for:

| Scenario | Action Required |
|----------|----------------|
| **Establishing** a business relationship with a PEP | Written approval before onboarding |
| **Continuing** an existing relationship with a PEP | Approval at each CDD review |
| **Existing customer/BO becomes** or is newly identified as PEP | Immediate approval for continuation |
| **Existing PEP relationship** is reviewed and CDD updated | Re-approval at review |
| **Material, unusual, or out-of-pattern** transactions involving a PEP | Approval before proceeding |

**This cannot be delegated.** Senior management must personally approve these relationships — the Compliance Officer can recommend, but the approval authority sits with senior management.

---

## Former PEPs

When a person is no longer in a political role, they are classified as a **former PEP**. However:

- Former PEP relationships are classified as **high-risk regardless** — the risk does not automatically decrease when the political role ends
- Ongoing monitoring continues
- The Compliance Officer determines when the risk level can be reassessed — typically not less than 12 months after leaving the political position, and often longer depending on the jurisdiction and role

## EDD for NPOs (Policy s.9.7.2)

Non-Profit Organisations receive additional EDD measures beyond the standard high-risk procedures:

| EDD Measure | What to Assess |
|-------------|---------------|
| **AML policies** | Does the NPO have its own AML/CFT policies and procedures? |
| **Regulatory status** | Is the NPO registered with and supervised by a regulatory body? |
| **Ownership/management structure** | Who runs the NPO? Any PEP involvement in governance? |
| **Activities** | What does the NPO actually do? Are activities consistent with stated mission? |
| **Donor base** | Where does funding come from? Any links to high-risk jurisdictions? |
| **Geographic areas** | Where does the NPO operate? Any conflict zones or high-risk areas? |

**Why NPOs require special attention:** NPOs can be exploited as channels for terrorist financing — funds donated to a legitimate charity can be diverted to support terrorism. This has been documented in multiple international typology studies.

## UBO Identification — Detailed Procedures

### The Legal Framework

- **Cabinet Resolution No. 58 of 2020** — Established the beneficial ownership framework
- **Cabinet Decision No. 109 of 2023** — Updated UBO definition and procedures

### The 25% Threshold

**UBO = any natural person who:**
- Owns **25% or more of the shares** (directly or indirectly), OR
- Controls **25% or more of the voting rights** (directly or indirectly)

### Indirect Ownership Calculation

When ownership passes through multiple entities:

**Example:**
- Person A owns 70% of Company X
- Company X owns 60% of Company Y (the buyer)
- Person A's indirect ownership of Company Y = 70% × 60% = **42%** → UBO

### The Cascade (When No One Meets 25%)

| Step | Test | Action |
|------|------|--------|
| 1 | **Aggregate ownership** | If multiple persons share ownership aggregating to ≥25%, all are treated as owners/controllers |
| 2 | **Control through other means** | Look for natural persons who exercise control through management, contracts, or other mechanisms |
| 3 | **Highest-ranking management** | If no natural person identified through Steps 1-2, the highest-ranking management official = UBO |

### When to Escalate

If each legal person or arrangement in the ownership chain holds less than 25%, but you suspect common ownership could aggregate to 25% or more, **escalate to the Compliance Officer**.

### UBO Exemptions

| Exemption | Condition |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Government-owned entities** | Directly or indirectly wholly owned by federal or emirate government |
| **Listed companies** | Listed on regulated stock exchange in Europe, US, UK, or GCC — must document evidence of exchange disclosure requirements |

## Key Takeaways

1. **PEP status extends to family and associates** — spouses, children, parents, siblings, and persons with joint beneficial ownership
2. **Three PEP categories** for screening: foreign, domestic, and international organisation (plus related persons)
3. **Senior management approval is mandatory** for establishing, continuing, or reviewing PEP relationships — and for material/unusual transactions
4. **Former PEPs remain high-risk** — PEP status does not expire automatically
5. **Cygnus Scan** screens against global PEP databases at onboarding, periodically, and on new alerts
6. **UBO identification uses the 25% rule** with a cascade when no natural person meets the threshold
7. **NPOs require additional EDD** covering their AML policies, governance, activities, donors, and geographic operations
