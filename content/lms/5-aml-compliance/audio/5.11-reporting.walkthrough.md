Let me walk you through the complete reporting chain — from the moment you develop a suspicion through the internal report, the Compliance Officer's review, to the external report filed on goAML. This is the process you will follow every time something does not look right.

---

## The Scenario

You are working with a new client, Mrs. Layla Mahmoud, a Lebanese national residing in Dubai. She is purchasing a AED 2.5 million apartment in Business Bay. During the KYC process, you notice the following:

1. She declares herself as a "housewife" with no independent income
2. The source of funds is listed as "family support" — but she cannot name which family member is providing the funds
3. She wants to pay the entire amount via bank transfer from an account in her name at a Lebanese bank
4. When you ask about source of wealth, she becomes evasive and says "my husband takes care of everything" but declines to provide his details
5. Lebanon is on the FATF grey list

You have multiple concerns. Let us walk through the reporting chain step by step.

---

## Step 1: Recognise the Indicators

Before filing anything, clearly identify what you are seeing:

**Red flags present:**
- Source of funds is vague and unverifiable ("family support" with no named family member)
- Evasive about source of wealth
- Refuses to provide husband's details (potential hidden beneficial interest)
- Funds from Lebanon (FATF grey list country)
- Transaction amount (AED 2.5 million) inconsistent with declared occupation (housewife with no income)

**Risk classification:**
- Lebanese national residing in UAE — check the risk table: not in the high-risk nationality list specifically, but FATF grey list country means enhanced scrutiny
- "Housewife/dependent" residing in UAE — medium risk per individual table
- However, multiple red flags elevate the concern beyond the baseline classification

---

## Step 2: File the ISTR

You decide the indicators warrant an internal report. You complete the ISTR form:

**Client name:** Layla Mahmoud
**Representative:** None
**Address:** Business Bay, Dubai (as per KYC form)
**Phone:** +971 50 XXX XXXX
**Email:** layla.m@gmail.com
**Business/Occupation:** Housewife — no declared independent income
**Passport:** Lebanese passport, number XXXXXXX
**Nationality:** Lebanese
**Business location:** N/A (housewife)

**Nature of suspected transactions:** Purchase of residential apartment, AED 2,500,000, paid via bank transfer from personal account at Lebanese bank

**Amount:** AED 2,500,000

**Connected accounts:** Personal account at [Lebanese bank name], possibly connected to husband's finances (details refused)

**Reason for suspicion:** This is the most important field. Be specific and factual:

"Client declares occupation as housewife with no independent income. Source of funds stated as 'family support' but client cannot or will not identify which family member is providing AED 2,500,000. When asked about source of wealth, client became evasive and stated 'my husband takes care of everything' but declined to provide husband's name, occupation, or financial details. Funds are to be transferred from a personal account at a Lebanese bank — Lebanon is a FATF grey list jurisdiction. The transaction amount (AED 2.5M) appears inconsistent with the client's declared socio-economic profile. Multiple red flags are present: vague source of funds, evasive about source of wealth, refusal to disclose connected persons, and funds from FATF grey list country."

**CO instructions:** Leave blank — the CO completes this.

**Timeline:** You submit this within 24 hours of establishing your suspicion. In this case, the suspicion crystallised during the KYC conversation — so the clock starts now.

---

## Step 3: Compliance Officer Review

The CO receives your ISTR and reviews the circumstances. She examines:

- Your documented red flags
- The KYC form (what has been completed and what is missing)
- Cygnus Scan screening results (sanctions, PEP, adverse media)
- Whether the transaction can be explained by innocent circumstances

The CO makes one of three determinations:

**Option A: No external report needed.** The CO documents why — for example, if additional information resolves the concerns. The ISTR is filed and retained, but no external report is made.

**Option B: Request additional information.** The CO asks you to go back to the client and obtain specific documents or answers before making a determination.

**Option C: External report required.** The CO determines that the suspicion warrants filing an STR with the FIU via goAML.

In this case, the CO determines: "Multiple unresolved red flags. Source of funds unverifiable. Potential third-party beneficial interest (husband). FATF grey list jurisdiction. Filing STR."

---

## Step 4: External Report — STR via goAML

The CO files the STR on the goAML platform within 24 hours of her determination. The STR includes:

- All information from the ISTR
- Screening results
- CO's analysis and rationale
- Transaction details
- Supporting documents (KYC form, whatever identification was provided)

---

## Step 5: Post-STR Actions

After the STR is filed:

**Immediate actions:**
1. Mrs. Mahmoud is reclassified as **high-risk immediately** — regardless of prior classification
2. **Enhanced monitoring** is implemented — all her activities are subject to heightened scrutiny
3. **Transaction execution is delayed where possible** — without tipping off. The CO may instruct you to slow the process by requesting additional documentation.

**Communication with the client:**
- You can continue normal professional communication
- You **cannot** mention the STR, the FIU, the investigation, or any compliance concern
- If Mrs. Mahmoud asks about delays, use neutral language: "We are finalising our compliance review. I will update you as soon as we have an answer."

**FIU instructions:**
- The FIU may request additional information — the firm is obliged to provide it promptly
- The FIU may instruct the firm to proceed, delay, or terminate the relationship
- Follow FIU guidance exactly

**Record retention:**
- All STR-related documents are retained for a minimum of 5 years from the closing of the investigation

---

## Alternative Scenarios

### What if the screening had returned a match?

If Cygnus Scan had returned a confirmed or partial name match for Mrs. Mahmoud against a sanctions list, the reporting would be different:

- Confirmed match → CNMR (not STR), freeze within 24 hours
- Partial match → PNMR (not STR), suspend immediately

The ISTR process is the same for you — you file internally regardless. But the CO's external report type changes based on whether it is suspicion-based (STR/SAR) or sanctions-based (CNMR/PNMR).

### What if the transaction involved cash ≥ AED 55,000?

If Mrs. Mahmoud had attempted to pay AED 55,000 or more in cash, a REAR would also be required — in addition to the STR if suspicion exists. REAR and STR are not mutually exclusive.

---

## Summary: The Complete Chain

```
You notice red flags
    ↓
File ISTR with CO (within 24 hours of suspicion)
    ↓
CO reviews circumstances
    ↓
CO determines: no report / request more info / file external report
    ↓
If STR required: CO files via goAML (within 24 hours of determination)
    ↓
Post-STR: reclassify as high-risk, enhance monitoring, delay if possible
    ↓
Follow FIU instructions, retain records for 5+ years
```

Your role in this chain is clear: recognise, document, and escalate. The CO handles the rest. But your ISTR — the quality, detail, and timeliness of your internal report — is what makes the entire chain work.
