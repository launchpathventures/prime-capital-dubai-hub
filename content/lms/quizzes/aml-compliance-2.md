## Questions

### Question 1
**Which UAE law is the primary AML/CFT legislation that establishes your obligations as a real estate professional?**

A) Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025
B) Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018
C) Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019
D) Cabinet Resolution No. 71 of 2024

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 is the primary AML/CFT legislation in the UAE — commonly referred to as "the Decree Law." It establishes definitions of ML/TF/PF offences, reporting obligations, penalties, and FIU powers. While Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019 provides implementing regulations, the Decree Law is the foundational legislation.

---

### Question 2
**Under which specific legal provision are real estate agents classified as DNFBPs?**

A) Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018, Article 2
B) Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019, Article 3
C) Cabinet Decision No. 74 of 2020, Article 1
D) Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, Article 5

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Cabinet Decision No. 10 of 2019, Article 3 specifically classifies real estate agents and brokers as Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs). This classification is the legal basis for all your AML/CFT obligations — CDD, reporting, record-keeping, and more.

---

### Question 3
**Which regulatory authority is the primary supervisor for DNFBPs in the UAE and can audit your AML compliance at any time?**

A) Central Bank of the UAE
B) Dubai Land Department (DLD)
C) Ministry of Economy
D) RERA

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Ministry of Economy is the supervisory authority for DNFBPs in the UAE. They oversee compliance by real estate agents, conduct inspections and audits, issue circulars and guidance, and impose administrative penalties for non-compliance. While RERA regulates real estate licensing, AML/CFT supervision falls under the Ministry of Economy.

---

### Question 4
**A new client is a national of Nigeria. According to the FATF lists referenced in Prime Capital's policy, what is Nigeria's current status?**

A) Blacklisted — the client is prohibited
B) Grey-listed (under increased monitoring) — enhanced scrutiny may be required
C) Not on any FATF list — standard procedures apply
D) Previously grey-listed but now removed — no additional measures needed

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** As of June 2025 (per Prime Capital's Policy Appendix II), Nigeria is on the FATF grey list (Jurisdictions Under Increased Monitoring). This means clients from Nigeria may require enhanced scrutiny and potentially EDD, depending on other risk factors. Grey-listed countries have strategic deficiencies but have committed to action plans — they are not prohibited like blacklisted countries.

### Question 5
**How many core AML/CFT obligations do DNFBPs have under UAE law?**

A) 4 — CDD, reporting, record-keeping, and training
B) 6 — CDD, risk assessment, record-keeping, reporting, TFS, and training
C) 8 — CDD, risk assessment, record-keeping, reporting, TFS, internal controls, training, and compliance programme
D) 10 — all of the above plus external auditing and customer notifications

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** DNFBPs have 8 specific obligations: (1) Customer Due Diligence, (2) Risk Assessment, (3) Record Keeping, (4) Suspicious Transaction Reporting, (5) Targeted Financial Sanctions compliance, (6) Internal Controls, (7) Training, and (8) maintaining a documented Compliance Programme with a designated Compliance Officer. All 8 obligations apply from day one — there are no exemptions.
