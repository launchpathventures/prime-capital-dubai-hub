# AML Compliance Quiz: PEPs & UBO Identification

**Purpose:** Verify your ability to identify Politically Exposed Persons, apply PEP-specific procedures, and determine Ultimate Beneficial Owners through ownership structures.

**Format:** 5 multiple-choice questions
**Passing Score:** 80% (4 out of 5 correct)
**Time Limit:** No time limit (typically 8 minutes)
**Retakes:** Unlimited

## Questions

### Question 1
**A client's brother is a foreign government minister. The client himself has no political role. Is the client a PEP?**

A) No — PEP status only applies to the person in the political role
B) Yes — siblings of PEPs are classified as PEP family members and all PEP procedures apply
C) Only if the client and the minister share financial assets
D) Only if the minister's country is on the FATF grey list

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** PEP status extends to direct and extended family members, including siblings. Under Prime Capital's PEP framework, siblings, uncles/aunts, and in-laws of PEPs are all classified as related persons. All PEP procedures apply — including EDD, Compliance Officer escalation, and senior management approval.

### Question 2
**A client was a government minister for 8 years but left office 6 months ago. They now operate a private consulting firm. What is their PEP status?**

A) No longer a PEP — they left public office
B) Former PEP — still classified as high-risk, with ongoing enhanced monitoring
C) Standard risk — they can be treated as any other self-employed individual after leaving office
D) PEP status expires after 12 months out of office

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Former PEPs remain classified as high-risk. PEP status does not automatically expire when the political role ends. The Compliance Officer determines when the risk level can be reassessed — typically not less than 12 months after leaving office, and often longer depending on the jurisdiction and the role held. Enhanced monitoring continues throughout this period.

### Question 3
**Who must provide approval before establishing a business relationship with a PEP at Prime Capital?**

A) The Compliance Officer
B) The line manager
C) Senior management — this responsibility cannot be delegated
D) Any member of the management team

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Senior management approval is mandatory for establishing any business relationship with a PEP. This responsibility cannot be delegated — the Compliance Officer can recommend, but the approval authority sits exclusively with senior management. This applies to new PEP relationships, continuation of existing ones, and material or unusual transactions involving PEPs.

### Question 4
**Company X is owned by three shareholders: Person A (20%), Person B (20%), and Person C (60%). Using the UBO identification rules, who qualifies as a UBO?**

A) All three — they are all shareholders
B) Only Person C — they exceed the 25% threshold
C) Person C is a UBO (60% exceeds 25%). Persons A and B individually do not meet 25%, but their combined 40% may require them to be treated as co-controllers — escalate to the Compliance Officer
D) No one — the 25% rule requires a single person to hold exactly 25%

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Person C clearly exceeds the 25% threshold and is a UBO. Persons A and B individually hold only 20% each (below 25%), but their combined holding is 40%. Under the cascade rules, if multiple persons share ownership that aggregates to 25% or more, they may need to be treated as owners/controllers. The correct action is to identify Person C as the definitive UBO and escalate the A+B aggregation question to the Compliance Officer.

### Question 5
**An NPO wants to purchase commercial property in Dubai. Beyond standard EDD, which additional factor must you specifically assess?**

A) The NPO's property management capabilities
B) The NPO's donor base and geographic areas of operation
C) The NPO's tax-exempt status documentation
D) The NPO's marketing materials and public communications

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** NPOs receive additional EDD measures including assessment of their donor base (where funding comes from, any links to high-risk jurisdictions) and geographic areas of operation (any conflict zones or high-risk areas). Other NPO-specific assessments include their own AML policies, regulatory/supervisory status, ownership/management structure (PEP involvement), and activities. NPOs require special attention because they can be exploited as channels for terrorist financing.
