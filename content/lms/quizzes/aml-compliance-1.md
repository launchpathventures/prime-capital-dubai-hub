## Questions

### Question 1
**A client is convicted of fraud but acquitted of the money laundering charges related to the same funds. Can they still be prosecuted for money laundering separately?**

A) No — acquittal of the predicate offence means ML charges cannot proceed
B) Yes — ML crimes are prosecuted independently of the predicate offence
C) Only if the fraud conviction is overturned on appeal
D) Only if new evidence of laundering emerges after the fraud trial

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Under UAE law, money laundering crimes are independent of the predicate offence. Punishment for the predicate offence does not prevent ML prosecution, and acquittal of the predicate offence does not automatically prevent ML prosecution. The two crimes are treated separately under the law.

---

### Question 2
**A wealthy businessman donates AED 500,000 from his legitimate business income to a charity that is later found to channel funds to a terrorist organisation. Which statement is most accurate?**

A) This is money laundering because the funds are being concealed
B) This is not a financial crime because the funds were earned legitimately
C) This could constitute terrorist financing — TF funds can come from legitimate sources
D) This is proliferation financing because it involves an international organisation

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** A critical distinction between ML and TF: money laundering always involves proceeds of crime (illicit source), but terrorist financing can involve funds from completely legitimate sources. What makes it TF is the knowledge or intention that the funds will be used to support terrorism — regardless of how the money was originally earned.

### Question 3
**During the "layering" stage of money laundering, what is the primary objective?**

A) Getting cash into the financial system for the first time
B) Creating distance between the funds and their criminal source through multiple transactions
C) Using the cleaned funds to purchase legitimate assets
D) Converting physical cash into digital currency

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Layering is the second stage of ML, where multiple transactions are used to disguise the connection between the funds and their criminal origin. In real estate, this often appears as complex corporate structures, multiple transfers between related entities, and transactions across jurisdictions. Placement (getting cash into the system) is Stage 1, and Integration (re-entering the legitimate economy) is Stage 3.

### Question 4
**Which of the following is NOT one of the reasons real estate is attractive for money laundering?**

A) High transaction values that can absorb large amounts of illicit cash
B) The ability to manipulate property prices (over- or under-valuation)
C) Government-mandated price transparency that makes all transactions public
D) Complex ownership structures that can obscure the true buyer

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** While Dubai does have transaction records through DLD, the lack of universal price transparency is actually one factor that can be exploited. The other options are all genuine reasons real estate is attractive for ML: high values absorb large sums, prices can be manipulated, and complex ownership structures (shell companies, trusts, nominees) can hide the real buyer. Additionally, international buyers, non-face-to-face transactions, and third-party payments create further opportunities.

### Question 5
**Money laundering poses four categories of risk to individuals and organisations. A Dubai brokerage has its banking relationships terminated after a client's transaction triggers an ML investigation. Which category of risk does this represent?**

A) Reputational risk
B) Financial risk
C) Legal risk
D) Operational risk

**Correct Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The termination of banking relationships constitutes operational risk — disruption to business operations. Without banking facilities, the brokerage cannot process transactions, receive commissions, or function normally. While reputational damage likely contributed to the bank's decision, the direct consequence (loss of banking services, disrupted operations) is an operational risk. The four ML risk categories are: reputational, financial, legal, and operational.
