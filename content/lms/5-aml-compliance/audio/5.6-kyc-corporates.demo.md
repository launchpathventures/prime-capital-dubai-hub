Let me show you two versions of handling corporate KYC. The scenario: a free zone company called Gulf Horizon Trading LLC wants to purchase a AED 12 million commercial property in Business Bay. The company's representative, Ms. Fatima Al-Rashid, presents a trade license and says she is the managing director.

---

[DEMO: Weak]

The consultant says: "Great, so Gulf Horizon Trading is buying the property. Let me get the company details. You are the managing director — can I get a copy of the trade license?"

Ms. Al-Rashid hands over the trade license. The consultant copies it and says: "And who owns the company?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "It is owned by a holding company. Gulf Horizon Holdings."

The consultant says: "OK, and who owns the holding company?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "That is a bit complicated. There are a few shareholders. I can send you the details later."

The consultant says: "Sure, just email them over when you can. For now, let me fill in what I have. The company is registered in JAFZA free zone, trade license is valid... and the source of funds?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "Company funds. We have the money available."

The consultant says: "Perfect. I think we have enough to get started. Let me arrange the viewing."

[COACH EXPLAINS]

This is a compliance failure at multiple levels.

First, the consultant accepted "a holding company" as the owner without investigating further. A free zone company owned by a holding company is exactly the kind of layered structure that requires thorough UBO tracing. Who owns the holding company? Who are the natural persons behind the structure?

Second, "I can send you the details later" is not acceptable. You cannot proceed with a transaction without completing UBO identification. The 25 percent threshold analysis must be done before any business activity.

Third, "company funds" is not an adequate source of funds response. For a AED 12 million purchase, you need to know which account, what type of funds, and you need documentary evidence — bank statements showing the available balance.

Fourth, free zone companies are classified as high-risk in Prime Capital's entity risk table. The consultant did not recognise this EDD trigger.

Fifth, the consultant said "I think we have enough to get started." They did not. They had a trade license and a vague answer. They need 12 documents and a complete UBO identification before proceeding.

---

[DEMO: Strong]

The consultant says: "Thank you for meeting me, Ms. Al-Rashid. Before we discuss the property, I need to complete our corporate onboarding process. This is more detailed than individual client onboarding — there are a number of documents I will need, and we need to establish the ownership structure of the company down to the individual level."

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "Of course. What do you need?"

The consultant says: "Let us start with the basics. I have the trade license — Gulf Horizon Trading LLC, registered in JAFZA. The license expires in September 2027. Can you confirm the core business activity?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "General trading — primarily construction materials and industrial supplies."

The consultant notes: general trading is classified as high-risk in Prime Capital's entity table. He says: "Thank you. Now, you mentioned you are the managing director. Can you tell me about the ownership structure? Who are the shareholders of Gulf Horizon Trading?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "The company is owned by Gulf Horizon Holdings Limited, which is registered in the DIFC."

The consultant says: "And who owns Gulf Horizon Holdings?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "It has three shareholders. Mr. Ahmad Nasser holds 50 percent, Mr. Khalid Ibrahim holds 30 percent, and the remaining 20 percent is held by Bright Future Investments, a company registered in the Cayman Islands."

The consultant draws a quick ownership chart and says: "Let me make sure I have this right. Gulf Horizon Trading is 100 percent owned by Gulf Horizon Holdings in DIFC. Gulf Horizon Holdings has three shareholders: Mr. Nasser at 50 percent, Mr. Ibrahim at 30 percent, and Bright Future Investments at 20 percent. Is that correct?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "Yes, that is correct."

The consultant says: "Mr. Nasser and Mr. Ibrahim both exceed the 25 percent threshold, so they are both UBOs and I will need their identification documents — passports, Emirates IDs if they are residents. For Bright Future Investments at 20 percent — they are below the 25 percent threshold individually, but I need to know who owns that entity. Can you provide that?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "Bright Future is owned by Mr. Nasser's brother — Omar Nasser. He holds 100 percent."

The consultant says: "So Omar Nasser indirectly owns 20 percent of Gulf Horizon Trading through Bright Future Investments. He does not individually exceed 25 percent. However, combined with Ahmad Nasser's 50 percent, the Nasser family controls 70 percent of the company. I will note this for our Compliance Officer to review — the aggregated family ownership is something we assess carefully."

The consultant continues: "Now, regarding the source of funds for the AED 12 million purchase. Which account will the funds come from?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "From Gulf Horizon Trading's operating account at Emirates NBD."

The consultant says: "I will need a recent bank statement showing the available balance, as well as the company's most recent audited financial statements or balance sheet. For a purchase of this size, we also need to understand the source of these funds within the business — are they from trading revenue, retained earnings, or another source?"

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "Trading revenue — the company has been operating for seven years. I can provide the audited accounts."

The consultant says: "Perfect. Let me give you a complete list of what I need." He reads from his checklist: "KYC form — I will send this to you. Source of fund undertaking — a signed declaration. The trade license, which I have. Memorandum of Association for Gulf Horizon Trading. VAT registration certificate. Passport and Emirates ID copies for Mr. Ahmad Nasser, Mr. Khalid Ibrahim, and yourself as the authorised representative. Address proof for the company — tenancy contract or utility bill. Bank statement showing available funds. Audited financial statements. Third-party documentation is not applicable since the company itself is purchasing. The AML questionnaire is not required since you are not a DNFBP. And finally, the UBO register — the official register showing beneficial owners."

He pauses. "I will also need the constitutional documents for Gulf Horizon Holdings and Bright Future Investments, and passport copies for Omar Nasser since he is part of the indirect ownership structure."

Ms. Al-Rashid says: "That is quite thorough."

The consultant says: "It is. Corporate purchases require more documentation than individual ones, especially for free zone entities, which our regulations classify as higher-risk. This is not a reflection on your company — it is standard procedure. Once I have everything, our compliance team will review and we can move forward."

[COACH EXPLAINS]

Here is what made this effective:

First, the consultant systematically traced the ownership chain. He did not stop at "a holding company" — he mapped three levels of ownership to identify every natural person with a stake.

Second, he recognised the aggregation issue. Ahmad Nasser (50%) and Omar Nasser (20% indirect) are family members whose combined ownership is 70%. This is exactly the kind of pattern the Compliance Officer needs to review.

Third, he specified exactly what source of funds documentation means. Not "company funds" — but which account, what type of revenue, and which financial statements to provide.

Fourth, he read through the complete 12-document checklist, adapting it for this specific situation (no AML questionnaire needed, no third-party docs). This ensures nothing is missed and sets clear expectations for the client.

Fifth, he explained why the process is thorough without being apologetic. "Free zone entities are classified as higher-risk" is a factual statement that normalises the additional scrutiny.

[KEY INSIGHT]

Corporate KYC is where many consultants fail — not from bad intent, but from accepting the first layer of information without digging deeper. The client who says "it is owned by a holding company" is not hiding anything — they are simply giving you the headline. Your job is to ask the follow-up questions that reveal the full picture. Every time.

[PRACTICE TIP]

Keep a UBO mapping template handy — a simple diagram where you can sketch ownership chains during meetings. It helps you spot gaps, aggregation issues, and missing information in real time, rather than discovering them after the client has left.
