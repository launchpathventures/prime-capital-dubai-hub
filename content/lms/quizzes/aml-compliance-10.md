## Questions

### Question 1
**A corporate client uses a Gmail address for all business communications, is registered at a shared mailbox address, and cannot be found on LinkedIn or any business directory. How many legal person red flags does this scenario contain?**

A) 1 — the Gmail address is unusual but not concerning on its own
B) 2 — the Gmail address and the shared mailbox address
C) 3 — public email domain, shared/mailbox address, and not findable online
D) 0 — these are common for small businesses

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Three distinct legal person red flags are present: (1) Using a public/non-professional email domain (Gmail) for corporate communications, (2) Registered at a shared address (mailbox service indicator), and (3) Not findable on internet or LinkedIn. Each of these individually warrants attention; together, they suggest the entity may not have genuine business operations. Apply EDD and escalate to the Compliance Officer.

### Question 2
**A client purchases a AED 5 million apartment, shows no interest in the property's construction quality, location amenities, or handover date, and does not negotiate the price despite it being above comparable market values. Which ML/FT typology does this most closely resemble?**

A) Smurfing/structuring — breaking amounts into smaller parts
B) Integration — laundered money used to purchase legitimate assets with no genuine investment interest
C) Family members as fronts — using nominees to purchase
D) Exploitation of charity — channelling funds through NPOs

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** This pattern matches the integration stage of ML: the client is using the property purchase to convert illicit funds into a legitimate asset. The red flags are: no interest in property characteristics (quality, construction, handover date), not interested in better price/payment terms, and accepting above-market pricing without negotiation. The client's goal is the transaction itself, not the property — a classic integration indicator.

### Question 3
**A client makes three separate cash payments of AED 18,000 each over three consecutive days to pay a booking deposit. What is this pattern called, and what should you do?**

A) Layering — report as an STR immediately
B) Structuring/smurfing — payments divided to stay below the AED 55,000 reporting threshold. Escalate to the Compliance Officer.
C) Normal behaviour — clients often make multiple payments
D) Integration — the funds are entering the property market

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** This is structuring (also called smurfing) — deliberately breaking a larger amount into smaller transactions to avoid triggering reporting thresholds. Three payments of AED 18,000 = AED 54,000, just below the AED 55,000 cash threshold that triggers REAR reporting. This is a clear red flag (payments divided into smaller parts with short intervals). Escalate to the Compliance Officer — the linked transactions total AED 54,000 and the pattern suggests deliberate avoidance of the threshold.

### Question 4
**In the typology case study "Family Members as Fronts," what was the key ML pattern?**

A) A family-owned business purchased property using legitimate business income
B) A Canadian with criminal connections purchased properties in his parents' names, repaying mortgages within 6 months using unexplained funds
C) Family members pooled legitimate savings to make a joint property investment
D) Parents gifted property to their children as part of estate planning

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** In Typology 7, a Canadian with criminal connections used his parents as nominees — purchasing properties in their names rather than his own. The mortgages were then repaid within 6 months using unexplained funds, inconsistent with the parents' financial profile. Red flags: family nominees, rapid mortgage repayment, source of funds inconsistent with the nominated owners' known income.

### Question 5
**When you identify multiple significant red flags during a client onboarding, what is the correct response?**

A) Apply EDD and proceed with the transaction if the client cooperates with documentation requests
B) Reject the client immediately — multiple red flags mean they are laundering money
C) Escalate to the Compliance Officer immediately and do not proceed until the CO provides guidance
D) Continue with standard CDD and note the flags in the file for the annual review

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Multiple significant red flags require immediate escalation to the Compliance Officer. You should not proceed with the transaction until the CO provides guidance. Red flags are indicators, not proof — so you should not reject the client unilaterally. But you also should not proceed as normal. The CO will assess the situation and determine whether to apply EDD, request additional documentation, file an ISTR, or decline the relationship.
