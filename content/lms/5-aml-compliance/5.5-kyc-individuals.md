# KYC for Individual Clients

## Introduction

Know Your Customer (KYC) is the foundation of AML compliance. It is the process by which you establish a client's identity, understand their circumstances, and assess the risk they present. For individual clients, KYC involves completing a detailed form, collecting supporting documents, and making an initial risk assessment.

**Critical point:** KYC is not a one-time event. It is an ongoing obligation that continues throughout the business relationship. You must update client information when circumstances change, and you must review CDD at the frequencies specified by their risk rating (every 6 months, annually, or every 2 years).

## Why This Matters

KYC is mandatory before any transaction. You cannot proceed with a property viewing, a listing agreement, or any other business activity on behalf of a client until satisfactory KYC has been completed. Attempting to transact without completed KYC is a compliance violation — regardless of the client's profile or the deal's value.

---

## The KYC Process Flow

Before diving into the form, understand the overall process:

1. **Identity Document Verification** — Collect and verify original, official, government-issued identification documents
2. **Customer Authentication** — Confirm the person is who they claim to be (match photo to person, verify document authenticity)
3. **Risk Assessment** — Classify the client using Prime Capital's risk rating tables (Module 5.4)
4. **Screening** — Run the client through Cygnus Scan for sanctions, PEP, and adverse media checks
5. **Documentation** — Complete the KYC form with all required fields and collect supporting documents
6. **Ongoing Monitoring** — Monitor for changes in circumstances, behaviour, or risk profile

### Key Principles

- **Understand the customer type** — individual, corporate, trust, etc.
- **Assess the channel** — face-to-face or non-face-to-face (non-face-to-face carries higher risk)
- **Evaluate the product type** — residential purchase, commercial investment, off-plan, etc.
- **Consider nationality** — affects risk classification and due diligence level
- **Check geographic presence** — where the client lives, works, and banks
- **Screen for sanctions indicia** — any indicators that the client may be sanctioned

## Individual KYC Form — Required Fields

Every individual client must complete a KYC form. The following fields are **all required**:

### Personal Information
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Full name (as per ID)** | Must match identification document exactly |
| **Father/spouse name** | For verification and relationship mapping |
| **Date of birth** | Must match ID document |
| **Place of birth** | Country and city |
| **Gender** | As per identification |
| **Nationality** | Affects risk rating directly |
| **Marital status** | Single, married, divorced, widowed |
| **Resident status** | Resident or non-resident — non-residents are automatically high-risk |

### Professional Information
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Occupation type** | Salaried, self-employed, professional, or other — affects risk rating |
| **Service type** | Sell, buy, leasing, or rent |

### Financial Information (Critical Fields)
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Source of Funds** | Where the money for THIS transaction is coming from (salary, savings, property sale, inheritance, etc.) |
| **Source of Wealth** | How the client accumulated their overall wealth (career earnings, business ownership, family wealth, etc.) |
| **Purpose of transaction** | Why the client is purchasing/selling (investment, personal use, residency, etc.) |

**Source of Funds vs Source of Wealth — The Key Distinction:**

- **Source of Funds** = Where is the money for this specific transaction coming from? (e.g., "proceeds from sale of my London flat" or "salary savings accumulated over 5 years")
- **Source of Wealth** = How did the client build their overall wealth? (e.g., "25 years as a surgeon" or "founded and sold a technology company in 2019")

Both questions must be answered. Source of wealth is especially important for PEPs and high-risk clients as part of EDD.

### Identification
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Emirates ID number** | For UAE residents |
| **Passport number** | For non-residents (or in addition to Emirates ID) |
| **Issue/expiry dates** | Documents must be current and valid |

### Contact Details
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **UAE address** | Required for residents |
| **Home country address** | Required for non-residents |
| **Mobile number** | Primary contact |
| **Email address** | For correspondence and documentation |

### Declarations
| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **PEP declaration** | Yes or no — if yes, must provide explanation of political function |
| **Source of fund undertaking** | Signed declaration confirming the legitimacy of funds |
| **General undertaking statement** | Signed acknowledgement of accuracy of information provided |

## Documents to Collect

For every individual client onboarding, you must collect **7 documents**:

| # | Document | Required For |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | **KYC form** (system-based or manual) | All clients |
| 2 | **Source of fund undertaking** (signed) | All clients |
| 3 | **Valid passport/visa/Emirates ID copy** | All clients |
| 4 | **Valid passport/visa/EID of POA holder** | If client uses a Power of Attorney |
| 5 | **Source of funds document** (bank statement, income proof, salary slip) | Cash transactions, PEPs, and high-risk clients (EDD) |
| 6 | **Source of wealth documentation** | PEPs and high-risk clients (EDD) |
| 7 | **Third-party undertaking letter + supporting docs** | If a third party is involved in the transaction |

### Document Verification Standards

- Use **original, official, government-issued documents** wherever possible
- For foreign nationals: documents must be **legally valid in the issuing jurisdiction**
- Copies must be clear and legible
- Expired documents are not acceptable — clients must provide current documentation
- If documents are in a language other than Arabic or English, certified translations may be required

## Handling PEP Declarations

When a client declares themselves as a PEP (or you identify PEP status through screening):

1. **Record the political function** — What position do/did they hold?
2. **Determine PEP category** — Foreign PEP, domestic PEP, international organisation PEP, or related person (family/associate)?
3. **Escalate to Compliance Officer** — All PEP relationships must be reviewed by the CO
4. **Apply EDD** — PEPs automatically require Enhanced Due Diligence
5. **Obtain senior management approval** — Mandatory before establishing the relationship
6. **Verify source of funds and source of wealth** — Required documentation, not just declarations
7. **Set enhanced monitoring** — More frequent CDD reviews (every 6 months)

**If a client initially declares "No" to PEP status but Cygnus Scan identifies them as a PEP:**
- Do not confront the client aggressively
- Inform the Compliance Officer
- The CO will determine next steps, which may include declining the relationship

## Professional Approach to KYC Questions

Many consultants find KYC questions uncomfortable — especially source of funds inquiries. Here is how to handle them professionally:

### Frame It as Standard Procedure
"As part of our regulatory obligations, I need to ask a few compliance questions. This is standard procedure for all clients — it is required by UAE law for all property transactions."

### Normalise the Process
"Every client goes through this process, regardless of the transaction size. It is how we ensure everything is properly documented and compliant."

### If the Client Pushes Back
"I understand this may feel intrusive, and I appreciate your patience. These requirements come from the Dubai Land Department and UAE federal law — they apply to every real estate transaction in Dubai. We cannot proceed without this information, but I assure you everything is handled confidentially."

### If the Client Refuses to Answer
- Do not pressure or argue
- Document the refusal
- Inform the Compliance Officer
- **Do not proceed with the transaction** — incomplete KYC means you cannot transact

## Non-Face-to-Face Clients

When a client is not physically present (they operate through a representative, POA holder, or remotely):

- **Higher risk by default** — non-face-to-face is an EDD trigger
- **POA verification** — collect valid identification for both the client AND the POA holder
- **Additional verification steps** may be required — video call verification, notarised documents, etc.
- The **same KYC requirements apply** to the actual client, not just the representative

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **KYC is mandatory before any transaction** — no exceptions
2. **KYC is ongoing** — not a one-time event; update when circumstances change
3. **7 documents** are required for individual onboarding — know the list
4. **Source of funds ≠ source of wealth** — funds = this transaction, wealth = overall accumulation
5. **PEP declarations** trigger EDD, CO review, and senior management approval
6. **Professional framing** makes KYC questions natural, not confrontational
7. **Client refusal to provide KYC** means you cannot proceed — document and escalate
