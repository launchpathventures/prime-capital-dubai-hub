# AML Compliance Quiz: Customer Due Diligence — SDD, CDD & EDD

**Purpose:** Verify your ability to apply the correct level of due diligence based on risk, understand CDD triggers, and identify when Enhanced Due Diligence is required.

**Format:** 5 multiple-choice questions
**Passing Score:** 80% (4 out of 5 correct)
**Time Limit:** No time limit (typically 8 minutes)
**Retakes:** Unlimited

## Questions

### Question 1
**A government-owned entity (low-risk) wants to purchase a commercial property. During onboarding, you notice an unusually large cash component and the representative seems evasive about the source. Which due diligence level should you apply?**

A) SDD — the entity is government-owned and low-risk
B) Standard CDD — the entity qualifies for SDD but cash warrants standard measures
C) EDD — suspicion of ML/TF overrides any low-risk classification, and SDD must never be applied when there is suspicion
D) No due diligence needed — government entities are exempt

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The absolute rule: SDD must NEVER be applied when there is any suspicion of ML/TF — regardless of the client's risk classification. An evasive representative and unusual cash component are suspicious indicators. Despite the entity being government-owned (normally eligible for SDD), the suspicious indicators require EDD. Escalate to the Compliance Officer immediately.

### Question 2
**Which of the following is NOT one of the 9 CDD measures Prime Capital implements?**

A) Screen against sanctions lists, international watch lists, and adverse media
B) Continuously monitor transactions against the client's risk profile
C) Guarantee the client's funds are from legitimate sources
D) Verify identity from original, government-issued documents

**Correct Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Prime Capital cannot "guarantee" the legitimacy of funds — you can verify, document, and assess, but guaranteeing is not a CDD measure. The 9 actual measures include: obtain legally valid documents, identify and verify, background screening, determine purpose, continuously monitor, scrutinise transactions, keep records current, exercise enhanced levels for higher risk, and verify from originals.

### Question 3
**A medium-risk client completed CDD 14 months ago. What is their review status?**

A) Current — medium-risk clients are reviewed every 2 years
B) Overdue — medium-risk clients must be reviewed annually, and this review is 2 months overdue
C) Due next month — the 15-month grace period has not expired
D) Only review if a trigger event occurs

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Medium-risk clients must have their CDD reviewed every 1 year (annually). At 14 months, the review is 2 months overdue. The review frequencies are: Low risk = every 2 years, Medium risk = every 1 year, High risk = every 6 months. Overdue reviews should be completed immediately and the delay documented.

### Question 4
**Which of the following clients does NOT automatically require Enhanced Due Diligence?**

A) A UAE resident salaried employee purchasing a residential apartment
B) A non-resident purchasing an investment property
C) A corporate entity purchasing through a holding company
D) A national of a FATF grey-list country

**Correct Answer:** A
**Explanation:** A UAE-resident salaried employee is typically low-to-medium risk and requires standard CDD (not EDD). All the other options are automatic EDD triggers: non-residents always require EDD, corporate customers require EDD, and nationals of FATF grey/blacklist countries require EDD. EDD is applied in addition to standard CDD, not as a replacement.

### Question 5
**Under what limited circumstances can you verify a client's identity AFTER establishing the business relationship?**

A) When the client is a VIP referred by senior management
B) When there is no suspicion of ML/TF, the risk is low, funds are held in escrow until verification is complete, and verification is a prerequisite to closing
C) When the transaction value is below AED 55,000
D) This is never permitted — identity must always be verified before any business relationship

**Correct Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The CDD timing exception allows post-relationship verification ONLY when all four conditions are met: (1) no suspicion of ML/TF, (2) ML/TF risk is assessed as low, (3) funds are held in escrow until verification is complete, and (4) verification is a prerequisite to closing. This exists to facilitate legitimate transactions where upfront verification would cause unnecessary disruption — but it does not reduce obligations.
